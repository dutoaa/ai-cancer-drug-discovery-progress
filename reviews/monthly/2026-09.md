## Field Progress

- AI systems are becoming more workflow-native, with outputs designed for clinical auditability, source tracing, and integration into existing data systems.
- Benchmarking is shifting from generic accuracy to clinically relevant metrics such as lesion-wise Dice, AUPRC, calibration, robustness across cohorts, and utility under label scarcity.
- Knowledge-guided representations are increasingly outperforming unconstrained feature discovery in small or noisy biomedical datasets.
- The field is moving toward multimodal biology, where images, omics, and longitudinal EHR data are combined or compared to improve prediction and mechanistic insight.
- There is growing attention to deployment constraints, including low-field MRI, HIPAA-compliant local LLM use, no-code interfaces, and computational efficiency.
- Validation standards are improving, with more papers reporting external cohorts, held-out registries, and sensitivity to data leakage or thresholding choices.

## Open Questions

- How well do agentic LLM systems generalize across hospitals, specialties, and data standards without extensive local reconfiguration?
- Which interpretability strategies are most useful to clinicians: symbolic rules, provenance traces, bottleneck concepts, or post hoc explanations?
- Can omics-augmented predictors consistently add value beyond established clinical and imaging biomarkers in prospective trials?
- What is the best way to evaluate AI tools when the clinical objective is workflow efficiency, not just classification accuracy?
- How can models be made robust to dataset shift across scanners, institutions, demographic groups, and evolving clinical coding practices?
- Which AI systems meaningfully improve decision-making or outcomes, rather than merely automating documentation or image processing?

## Outlook

The September 2026 literature suggests AI in medicine is entering a consolidation phase: the novelty is no longer just model scale, but trustworthiness, transferability, and clinical utility. The most promising work combines strong validation with actionable integration into real workflows, especially in oncology. Over the next year, the key test will be whether these systems can move from retrospective benchmarks to prospective deployment with measurable benefit, while maintaining transparency, safety, and fairness.
