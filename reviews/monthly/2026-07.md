## Field Progress

- Clinical AI is increasingly framed as decision support rather than pure prediction, with outputs such as treatment rankings, risk staging, and modality acquisition policies.
- Multimodal learning is becoming more structured, using graphs, retrieval, temporal alignment, and knowledge priors to represent patient context more faithfully.
- Self-supervised and foundation-model pretraining is now central in imaging, especially for CT and pathology, where labeled data remain limited.
- Evaluation quality is improving in some subfields through external validation, prospective cohorts, and benchmarks that expose shortcut learning and domain bias.
- Explainability is evolving from feature importance toward evidence grounding, mechanistic interpretation, and alignment with clinical workflows.
- Therapeutic discovery is broadening to include not just generation but synthesis planning, docking, structure prediction, and mass-spectrometry-based elucidation.

## Open Questions

- How can multimodal medical AI systems be calibrated and validated prospectively before clinical deployment across hospitals and populations?
- What is the best way to combine foundation-model representations with explicit clinical priors without sacrificing robustness or interpretability?
- How should agentic LLM systems be audited for safety, hallucination, and accountability when they produce treatment guidance or severity assessments?
- Can cross-domain generalization be made reliable enough for imaging models trained on one institution to transfer to another without performance collapse?
- Which explanation formats are most useful to clinicians: saliency maps, retrieved evidence, mechanistic graphs, or textual rationales?
- How can therapeutic discovery models be linked more directly to experimental feedback loops and downstream translational success?

## Outlook

The next phase of AI medicine will likely center on clinically grounded systems that integrate multiple data types, reason over time, and operate under realistic workflow constraints. The papers this month suggest that technical novelty is increasingly expected to come with external validation, robustness analysis, and some form of interpretability or operational utility. Progress is strongest where models are tied to concrete clinical decisions or molecular design tasks, but the field still needs better prospective evidence, standardized benchmarks, and safety frameworks before these methods can be trusted at scale.
