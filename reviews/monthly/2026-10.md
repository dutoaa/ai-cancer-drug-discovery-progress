## Field Progress

- Clinical validation designs are becoming more demanding: locked-model trial evaluation, independent regional cohorts, and explicit cross-cohort testing provide more informative evidence than random internal splits alone.
- Spatial and multimodal modeling is connecting morphology with biological context. AlphaFISH profiles host–microbiome organization in 149 colorectal biopsies from 68 people, while REST, VGL-stGen, and HistoLoop explore complementary approaches to linking histology and gene expression.
- Language-model applications are becoming more operationally specific, including registry abstraction, typed record-based judgments, eligibility queries, and clinician-supervised planning rather than unrestricted clinical dialogue.
- Robustness is increasingly treated as a primary objective through missing-modality segmentation, methylation-based out-of-distribution detection, data-efficient pathology, and cross-domain imaging transfer.
- Evaluation is becoming more skeptical and mechanistic: studies distinguish diagnosis from longitudinal management, localization from explanation faithfulness, and molecular binding from selectivity. Evidence of improved patient outcomes remains limited in the supplied material.

## Open Questions

- Do spatial pathology biomarkers improve calibration, treatment selection, and patient outcomes beyond established clinicopathological and genomic tools in prospective external cohorts?
- Can longitudinal imaging models distinguish treatment effects from baseline prognosis, acquisition differences, and treatment-selection confounding?
- How reliably do clinical language models preserve temporal evidence boundaries, recognize insufficient information, and defer across the full care pathway?
- Which privacy-aware deployment strategies retain utility across institutions, and how should privacy protection, extraction errors, computational cost, and monitoring be evaluated together?
- Can histology-derived molecular predictions and generated or imputed MRI preserve clinically actionable biology without introducing plausible but misleading features?
- Do gains on target-retrieval, selectivity, and molecular-generation benchmarks translate into experimentally verified activity, reduced off-target effects, and better development decisions?

## Outlook

The next meaningful advances should come from combining stronger representations with stronger evidence: prospective workflow studies, locked external validation, calibrated uncertainty, explicit failure detection, and comparisons against current clinical practice. Spatial pathology and privacy-aware record processing appear especially relevant to near-term evaluation, while longitudinal world models and autonomous discovery agents need clearer demonstrations of reliability and added value. Across imaging, language, and molecular design, success should increasingly be judged by decision quality, experimental confirmation, and patient benefit rather than benchmark performance alone.
