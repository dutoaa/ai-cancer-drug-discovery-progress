Emma Sarfati, Alexandre Bône, Pietro Gori, Sisi Yang, Marc-Michel Rohé, François Nicolas

Early diagnosis of hepatocellular carcinoma is critical to preserve access to curative treatment options. The Liver Imaging Reporting and Data System (LI-RADS) provides a standardized framework for non-invasive diagnosis on multiphase computed tomography, based on major visual imaging features. However, these features may be difficult to assess consistently, particularly in small liver lesions. On the external test set, sensitivities were similarly compared. dLI-RADS was non-inferior to LI-RADS for lesion-level accuracy in the internal evaluation set, achieving 80% accuracy compared with 74% for LI-RADS (p = 0.04)...

Multimodal profiling for prediction of primary resistance to anti-PD-(L)1 therapy in advanced non-small-cell lung cancer: the prospective PIONeeR biomarkers study

BackgroundPretreatment prediction of primary resistance to anti-PD-(L)1 therapy in advanced non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) remains an unmet clinical need. Existing biomarkers -- including PD-L1 expression and tumour mutational burden (TMB) -- are insufficiently discriminatory, and multimodal predictive models targeting primary resistance are still lacking to precisely drive patients treatment strategy. MethodsPIONeeR was a prospective, multicentre biomarker study conducted across 17 centres (NCT03493581). Adults with advanced NSCLC initiating standard-of-care first-line platinum-based chemotherapy plus anti-PD-(L)1 therapy, or second-or-later-line anti- PD-(L)1 monotherapy, were enrolled. Pretreatment multimodal profiling spanned six biological layers: clinical, routine medical biology, high-dimensional circulating immune phenotyping, soluble vascular markers, tumour immune contexture with digital pathology analysis of multiplex immunohistochemistry and immunofluorescence, transcriptomics and genomics. ResultsBetween March 2018 and January 2023, 439 patients were enrolled (269 first-line, 170 subsequent-line); 435 were evaluable for PrR, which occurred in 39.5%. The 18-feature signature included stromal regulatory T-cell infiltration, circulating transitional B cells, and multiple routine laboratory variables, with treatment-setting-dependent contributions. InterpretationDespite comprehensive multimodal pretreatment profiling, primary resistance to anti- PD-(L)1 therapy in advanced NSCLC cannot be captured by isolated biomarkers alone. Complete results are publicly accessible through an interactive dashboard (https://compo.inria.fr/pioneer-dashboard/), a reference resource for future meta-analyses. Multimodal integration improved risk stratification while highlighting the strong contribution of routinely available baseline variables. These findings support the development of clinically pragmatic models to identify patients who should access next-generation immunotherapy strategies aimed at overcoming primary resistance to PD-(L)1 inhibition.

Reading the Mask: Descriptor-Grounded BI-RADS Rationale Generation for Breast MRI Segmentation

Hrishikesh Singh, Aarti Sharma, Divyanshu Kumar, Millie Pant

Deep learning based models accurately delineate tumours on dynamic contrast-enhanced MRI (DCE-MRI), but segmentation masks alone do not provide the rationales or reasoning used by radiologists to assess the lesion suspicion. This limits the explainability as well the clinical integration. We investigate whether clinically relevant descriptors can instead be derived from the segmentation masks and their dynamic contrast-enhancement series. We have used Breast Imaging Reporting and Data System (BI-RADS) guidelines as a structured framework. Using these guidelines we designed a BI-RADS decision tree with eight quantitative measurements using radiomics, together with the lesion size and component count, that determine the descriptors at eight nodes. Using these descriptors we generate natural-language rationale using a large language model. The model was constrained to express only the derived records from the decision tree. After applying this framework to the expert and automatic masks of each of the 1,506 cases in the MAMA-MIA dataset, we generated a reproducible corpus of 3,012 BI-RADS grounded rationales. We obtained a faithfulness score of 99.98% and a counterfactual accuracy of 99.3%, indicating that the generated rationales faithfully verbalises the measured descriptors and correctly reflects changes in the underlying record. Agreement between the descriptors from expert and automatic masks falls from 0.832 to 0.549 as mask quality falls from Good to Missed. The generated rationales are intended as draft representations for radiologist review and final sign-off rather than autonomous clinical reports.

VGL-stGen: Learning a shared multimodal model for lung histology and spatial transcriptomics

Haenara Shin, Dongjoo Lee, Sungwoo Bae, Kwon Joong Na, Daeseung Lee, Hongyoon Choi

VGL-stGen is a multimodal large language model adapted from a pretrained medical vision-language model using paired lung H&E and spatial transcriptomics with single-cell references. Training integrates image-expression and expression-cell-type pairs, and gene-conditioned pseudo-labels transfer transcriptomic cell-type supervision to histology images. One deployed checkpoint supports thirteen evaluated tasks spanning tissue and cell-type classification, gene prediction and conditional expression completion from combinations of histology, cell identity and observed gene expression. Task-specific decoding produces categorical labels, gene lists and structured expression levels. For conditional completion, fixing queried gene identities and predicting their levels autoregressively improves structured prediction. Controlled adaptation shows that restricting supervision to requested expression-level tokens can further improve image-conditioned completion. Image-permutation experiments indicate that correctly paired histology adds information beyond observed expression. External classification relies partly on computational references with shared annotation lineage, and some H&E sources were exposed during cancer training or model development. Within these conditions, VGL-stGen demonstrates how a medical vision-language backbone can learn from spatial transcriptomics and histology to connect tissue morphology and molecular states through shared generative modeling.

REST: Reliability-Guided Spatial Transcriptomics Supervision for Gene Expression Prediction from Histopathological HE-Stained Images

Lanfeng Zhong, Xiangyu Sun, Guixuan Xu, Rongzhao Zhang, Jie Xu, Shaoting Zhang

Histopathology is central to cancer diagnosis, but morphology in Hematoxylin and Eosin (HE)-stained images does not directly reveal the molecular states that characterize disease heterogeneity and inform tumor characterization, prognostic assessment, and biomarker discovery. Spatial Transcriptomics (ST) measures spatially resolved gene expression, providing molecular information within tissue architecture, yet differences in assay coverage and spot informativeness make paired HE--ST observations unequally reliable for representation learning. We propose Reliability-Guided Spatial Transcriptomics (REST) supervision with Biological Program Bank (BPB), a program-structured and reliability-guided ST-supervised training framework for predicting molecular information from H\&E images. REST constructs BPB from curated signature, pathway, and cell-type gene sets to provide a shared structure for ST encoding, reliability estimation, and molecular reconstruction. Guided by BPB, Spot-Reliability Contrastive Alignment (SRCA) measures the reliability of each spot from assay-level program coverage and spot-level activity dispersion, and reweights HE--ST alignment accordingly. Biology-Informed Program Reconstruction (BIPR) further uses learnable program queries to reconstruct member-gene expression from histological morphology. REST's training corpus comprised 209 samples (407,475 spots across 16 organs); evaluation used 70 disjoint samples (230,056 spots across eight organs) for gene-expression, pathway-activity, and cell-signature prediction. REST achieved the highest Macro correlation among state-of-the-art ST-supervised training methods and outperformed existing pathology foundation models. Code is available at https://github.com/lanfz2000/REST.

CAPS: Clinically-Verifiable Anatomy-Prioritized Synthesis for Missing MRI Imputation in Brain Tumor Segmentation

Cecilia Diana-Albelda, Arthur Longuefosse, Mario Rubén Paz Campos, Álvaro García-Martín, Jesus Bescos

Multi-parametric MRI is the gold standard for neuro-oncology, yet acquiring complete 4-modality protocols may be unfeasible in real-world clinical practice. When handling missing modalities, existing automated systems force a critical compromise: robust segmenters operate as uninterpretable "black boxes" that bypass image generation, while standard synthesis models often produce blurry reconstructions lacking diagnostic reliability. To address this issue, we propose CAPS, a Clinically-Verifiable Anatomy-Prioritized Synthesis framework that reconciles computational precision with clinical transparency. Our multi-modal generative architecture is driven by a hybrid objective function (L_1 + L_AFP + L_DualDice) that explicitly enforces the preservation of deep anatomical features and strict topological boundaries, rather than relying solely on pixel-wise optimization. Evaluated comprehensively on the BraTS Adult Glioma dataset, the framework achieves superior synthesis fidelity across intensity-based metrics, and demonstrates robust downstream segmentation performance (82.38% mean Dice), successfully restoring verifiable visual context. Furthermore, the framework exhibits exceptional zero-shot cross-domain generalization, maintaining high structural fidelity across completely unseen Meningioma, Pediatric, and Sub-Saharan African cohorts without domain-specific adaptation. Finally, a blinded radiological assessment confirms that our anatomy-aware approach significantly enhances both perceived image quality and clinical utility, providing clinicians with the reliable visual evidence required to trust downstream automated workflows.

An Evidence-Driven Multi-Agent Compound AI System for Head and Neck Proton Radiotherapy Planning

Qingqing Wang, Liqiang Xiao, Chang Chang

Radiation treatment planning requires balancing competing objectives to satisfy clinical criteria while accommodating physicians' preferences. This study aims to develop an evidence-driven, multi-agent compound AI system that integrates DRL-based automated treatment planning with LLM-based conversational plan refinement for proton PBS therapy. A policy network trained using PPO first generates multiple plans without human intervention, from which one is selected as the target plan and the others serve as references for subsequent refinements. For challenging cases requiring further adjustments, an LLM-based multi-agent system enables plan refinement through natural language interactions. The system comprises a planning agent that recommends evidence-supported adjustments, a chatbot providing a unified conversational interface, and an evaluation agent that retrieves clinical metrics and identifies constraint violations. To enhance domain-specific reasoning, the planning agent based on Qwen3-14B is designed to justify its recommendations using evidence from previously generated plans and post-trained on task-specific data using SFT and GRPO. The framework was trained and evaluated using 21 patients with H&N cancers, with 5 PPO-generated plans per patient. Of these, 50 plans were selected for instruction-guided refinement. Compared with the base LLM, the post-trained Qwen3-14B demonstrated improved instruction-following, information retrieval and domain-specific reasoning capabilities. The resulting adjustments more effectively improved user-prioritized objectives while limiting deterioration in competing objectives, yielding more clinically appropriate trade-offs in patient-specific plan refinement. The proposed compound AI system provides a user-friendly planning platform, offering a promising approach to personalized proton PBS treatment planning.

Convergent Performance, Divergent Mechanisms in LoRA-Adapted Vision Foundation Models for Brain MRI

Cecilia Diana-Albelda, Fiona Kekwick, Raghav Mehta, Aleksandra Kulbaka, Paul M. Matthews, Jesus Bescos

Adapting Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) to medical imaging via LoRA has become standard practice, yet model selection is guided almost entirely by downstream task metrics, treating backbones with similar performance as interchangeable. We investigate whether equal performance implies equal internal representational shift. We analyse four ViT-B/16 backbones, DINOv3, SigLIP2, MerMED-FM, and BiomedCLIP, pre-trained with different objectives (self-distillation vs. contrastive vision-language) and on different data domains (natural vs. medical images). We adapt each to brain tumour segmentation and evaluate on six datasets covering both segmentation and Alzheimer's disease classification. Using attention divergence, weight-change analysis, and activation patching, we find that the four models converge to similar segmentation performance (mean Dice 78-81%) through markedly different internal dynamics, yet diverge in cross-task transfer: segmentation adaptation improves Alzheimer's disease classification for DINOv3 but degrades it for the other three backbones. We reveal a dissociation between modification magnitude and causal importance: the most heavily modified layers are not the causally relevant ones, with early-to-mid layers identified as sufficient for segmentation recovery. Restricting LoRA to these layers matches or approaches full-layer segmentation while reducing classification degradation in the three affected backbones, providing a mechanistically grounded strategy that is more parameter-efficient and more robust than standard full-layer adaptation.

Pathologic complete response (pCR) after neoadjuvant chemotherapy is a key prognostic indicator in aggressive breast cancer, yet existing prediction approaches either rely on pretreatment imaging alone, missing the patient's evolving response, or optimize multi-visit classifiers under direct outcome supervision, coupling representation learning with the clinical endpoint. We formulate longitudinal pCR prediction as a world-modeling problem and propose Conditioned Response-state World Modeling (CoRe-WM), which learns a latent state-transition model that predicts the next imaging state from the observed sequence. The transition function is conditioned on patient clinical profile and assigned therapy through additive shift and feature-wise modulation, enabling patient- and treatment-specific dynamics. A Factorized Response State pathway further decouples treatment-response dynamics from imaging appearance by encoding the structural prior that response manifests primarily in lesion morphology. After pretraining, all parameters are frozen and a linear readout predicts pCR at each clinical decision point. We evaluate CoRe-WM on I-SPY2 under five-fold cross-validation and assess cross-cohort transfer on I-SPY1. CoRe-WM achieves the highest discrimination among all evaluated methods at every prospective decision point, and demonstrates robust cross-cohort transfer. Ablation studies identify patient-profile conditioning and the factorized response pathway as the principal contributors. These results establish world-model-based representation learning as a promising framework for outcome prediction in oncology and beyond.

Expression-derived clustering further suggests that HistoLoop better captures spatially organized multigene patterns.

StochIKM: Automated High-Fidelity Reconstruction of Individual Patient Data from Published Kaplan-Meier Curves

Liangze Ke, Daniel Zelterman, Wei Wei, Guannan Gong

Individual patient data are the gold standard for secondary analysis of time-to-event evidence, but they are rarely released, and reconstruction from a published Kaplan-Meier figure could help. Existing methods all claim high fidelity, each judged by whichever agreements it happens to achieve, and each returns a single dataset although the map from figure to data is not bijective. We define fidelity explicitly: a dataset is admissible when it reproduces the risk table exactly, follows the digitized curve to within the image resolution, and respects the printed median, interval and event count. We propose StochIKM, a stochastic algorithm that digitizes the figure with a vision-capable language model and uses the high-fidelity definition as constraints, returning an admissible set of individual patient datasets. A solution is guaranteed to exist whenever the figure has been read correctly, so a failure indicts either the published figure or its digitization. In simulation and in 79 arms from published oncology trials, StochIKM reproduces the published risk tables and medians more closely than LLM-improved IPDfromKM, a high-impact alternative.

Generative Retrieval for Reverse Target Fishing with a Proteome-Scale Benchmark

Zhichao Deng, Haichuan Tan, Xin Hong, Huaizhi Wang, Tao Huang, Bowen Gao

Reverse target fishing identifies protein targets for a query molecule and is central to drug discovery and repurposing. Discriminative approaches adapted from virtual screening face sparse, long-tailed associations and missing-not-at-random measurements: treating unrecorded pairs as negatives can suppress genuine targets. We reformulate the task as generative retrieval and introduce GenFish. Residual vector quantization converts frozen protein language model features into shared semantic identifiers. Given a molecular fingerprint, GenFish autoregressively models codes conditioned on the query and preceding code, ranking proteins by identifier likelihood without explicitly labeling unrecorded pairs as negatives. We introduce ProTargetBench, the first proteome-scale benchmark for reverse target fishing to jointly support measured-panel discrimination and full-catalogue recovery of experimentally recorded positives. Covering 20,431 UniProt-reviewed human proteins, it integrates curated bioactivity evidence and distinguishes experimental inactivity from unmeasured associations. On 198 held-out molecules, GenFish achieves higher mean performance across all three full-catalogue retrieval metrics than four discriminative neural baselines and three non-neural baselines, including ligand-based methods. A controlled positive-hiding experiment shows that targeted removal of false-negative competition improves contrastive retrieval beyond matched random masking; GenFish remains stronger under the same incomplete supervision. Pfam alignment indicates that the semantic identifiers capture biologically meaningful relationships among proteins. These results establish generative retrieval as a principled paradigm for reverse target fishing under incomplete bioactivity records.

A general-purpose System One decision model for clinical records

Andrew Zhang, Trinh T. L. Vuong, Sophia J. Wagner, Dongmin Bang, Tong Ding, Yuchang Su

Clinical care, health-system operations and biomedical discovery depend on extracting reliable answers from patient records, yet applying diverse clinical questions across entire populations remains computationally expensive. Here we present Clinical System One (CS1), a 4-billion-parameter decision model for fast, typed judgments from clinical records, along with two agentic harnesses in which CS1 works together with a generalist language model.
