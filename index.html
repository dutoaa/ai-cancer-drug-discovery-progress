Can GPT Be Used as an Alternative Prediction Model to Traditional Machine Learning and Neural Networks on Low-Volume Clinical Data?

Bin Akter, S.; Akter, S.; Eisenberg, D.; Hill, C.; Lotvola, A.; Fresneda Fernandez, J.; Sarkar Pias, T.; Rafiqul Islam, M.; Islam, H.

Background and Objective: Early and reliable disease prediction from structured clinical data remains challenging when datasets are small, highly imbalanced, and contain limited positive disease cases. Conventional machine learning (ML) and deep learning approaches often struggle to capture clinically meaningful relationships under such low-data representation conditions due to weak statistical associations between features and prediction targets. This study proposes a clinically grounded GPT2-based table-to-text framework for disease prediction using structured healthcare datasets, motivated by the contextual reasoning capability of GPT models to better capture clinically meaningful relationships when statistical learning alone becomes insufficient due to limited data availability. Methods & Materials: Structured clinical records were transformed into physician-style textual descriptions and enriched through GPT4-generated medical paraphrasing to improve minority-class representation while preserving clinical meaning. Both the original and generated clinical texts were used to fine-tune a GPT2 model across four public healthcare datasets, including heart disease, heart failure, chronic kidney disease, and thyroid cancer recurrence. Gradient-based explainable AI analysis was additionally incorporated to identify clinically important features influencing prediction outcomes. Results: The proposed framework demonstrated consistently strong predictive performance with average precision, specificity, sensitivity, and F1-score of 0.96, 0.97, 0.96, and 0.96, respectively. The model achieved improved sensitivity, stronger generalization, and more stable predictive behavior compared with traditional ML, deep learning, transformer-based, and GAN-augmented approaches. Importantly, the framework consistently emphasized clinically meaningful variables even under severe class imbalance where conventional ML and neural network models often struggled. Conclusions: The proposed GPT2-based table-to-text framework provides a practical and clinically interpretable approach for disease prediction from limited structured healthcare data. By integrating contextual clinical reasoning with explainable prediction mechanisms, the framework demonstrates strong potential for early risk detection, transparent clinical decision support, and reliable deployment in real-world low-resource healthcare environments.

COVID-19 vaccination and survival of cancer patients in Northwestern Russia: a historical registry-based cohort study

Dyachenko, A. A.; Bogdanov, M. A.; Bogdanov, D. V.; Meldo, A. A.; Chernina, V. Y.; Gombolevskiy, V. A.; Grjibovski, A. M.; Valkov, M. Y.

BackgroundCancer patients face a significantly elevated risk of severe COVID-19 outcomes. Although vaccination is a cornerstone of prevention, data on vaccination coverage and association with survival among Russian cancer patients remain scarce. We assessed COVID-19 vaccination coverage and its association with overall survival in a population-based cohort of cancer patients in Northwest Russia. MethodsThis retrospective registry-based cohort study integrated data from the Population-Based Cancer Registry (PBCR) of the Arkhangelsk Region and the Nenets Autonomous Okrug, regional vaccination records, and an artificial intelligence (AI)-based analysis of chest computed tomography (CT) scans. The cohort consisted of 1,144 cancer patients who underwent CT between August 2020 and April 2021. Vaccination status was ascertained via data linkage. Overall survival (OS) was compared between vaccinated and unvaccinated patients using Kaplan-Meier analysis and multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression, adjusted for demographic, clinical, and AI-derived CT biomarkers. To mitigate immortal time bias, the primary analysis used a landmark approach restricted to patients alive on March 1, 2021 (N = 1,136). A sensitivity analysis used a November 1, 2021 landmark (N = 894). Vaccination status was defined as receipt of at least one dose before the landmark date. ResultsOnly 6.6% (n=76) of patients received at least one dose of a COVID-19 vaccine (Sputnik V or Sputnik Light). Vaccinated patients were more likely to have earlier-stage cancer and were less likely to be receiving active treatment. In the primary landmark analysis, vaccination was associated with a significantly higher 3-year OS (80.3% vs. 45.7%, p < 0.001). In multivariable analysis, vaccination was independently associated with a 60% lower hazard of all-cause mortality (adjusted HR 0.40, 95% CI 0.25-0.64). AI-detected COVID-19 pneumonia on baseline CT was also an independent predictor of poorer survival (adjusted HR 1.32, 95% CI 1.09-1.59). In the sensitivity analysis (November 1, 2021 landmark), the adjusted HR for vaccination was 0.56 (95% CI 0.34-0.92), showing a consistent but attenuated association. ConclusionThis study reveals critically low COVID-19 vaccination coverage among cancer patients in Northwest Russia. Nevertheless, vaccination was strongly associated with a substantial survival benefit, underscoring its importance as a potentially lifesaving intervention for this vulnerable population.

Developing an OMOP-Standardized Prostate Cancer Database and Improving Data Quality Using NLP and PSA-Based Algorithms

Wang, J.; Jackson, J. C.; Garza, A.; Nalla, S.; Ninnemann, T.; Zhang, Y.; Kuo, Y.-F.

ObjectiveTo develop and evaluate an Observational Medical Outcomes Partnership-(OMOP-) standardized prostate cancer database from the University of Texas Medical Branch (UTMB) Epic Electronic Health Record (EHR) and improve data quality using natural language processing(NLP) and prostate-specific antigen-(PSA) based algorithms. Materials and MethodsWe built a data pipeline to transform UTMB Epic EHR data for patients identified through the Galveston Cancer Registry (2010-2021) into OMOP Common Data Model (CDM) v5.4. Data quality was assessed by comparing the OMOP-standardized data with Galveston Cancer Registry data using availability agreement, Cohens kappa and Intraclass Correlation Coefficient. NLP was used to extract PSA, Gleason score, and cancer stage from clinical text, and PSA-based algorithms were used to identify missing treatment and biochemical recurrence. ResultsWe extracted 815 analytic cases from UTMB EHR. Among them, 700 (85.9%) were complete and concordant with the cancer registry. PSA showed excellent value agreement. Structured Gleason score and stage data were sparse (n<20), but NLP greately improved capture. Treatment agreement was good compared with the cancer registry and improved slightly for radical prostatectomy after applying a PSA-based algorithm. Using PSA trajectories, we identified 60 cases of biochemical recurrence. DiscussionThe OMOP-standardized data from UTMB showed good agreement with the cancer registry. However, structured EHR fields incompletely captured diagnosis, pathology, and treatment details. NLP and PSA-based algorithms substantially improved data capture. Manual review also revealed errors in registry data, showing that OMOP-standardized EHR data can complement and help improve cancer registry quality. Conclusion: OMOP standardization combined with NLP and PSA-based algorithms improved prostate cancer data quality and research readiness. Lay SummaryElectronic health record (EHR) systems contain valuable long-term health information about cancer, but their use is limited by difficulties in data extraction, the lack of standard data formats, and missing information in structured fields. In this study, we built a data pipeline to convert EHR data into the Observational Medical Outcomes Partnership (OMOP) Common Data Model, a widely used standard format that facilitates data sharing and collaborative research. To address missing data, we used natural language processing (NLP, a computational technique that enables computers to interpret human language) to extract prostate-specific antigen (PSA) levels, Gleason scores (a measure of cancer aggressiveness), and cancer stages from unstructured clinical notes. We also applied algorithms that track PSA blood trends over time to identify unrecorded cancer treatments and detect cancer recurrence. Comparing this database with a cancer registry demonstrated that standardized EHR records provide accurate clinical information and long-term follow-up. While structured EHR fields lacked key tumor characteristics, NLP dramatically improved their capture. Tracking PSA trends identified previously unrecorded treatments and flagged 60 potential cancer recurrences. Overall, combining standardized EHR formats with NLP and PSA-based algorithms creates a rich database to support real-world prostate cancer research.

Language eligibility criteria and enrolled racial and ethnic composition in US clinical trials: a cross-sectional study

Tolas, A.; Du, L. Y.; Schutz, N.; Schmiedmayer, P.; O'Sullivan, J. W.; Johnson, A.; Longenecker, C. K.; Sarraju, A.; Rodriguez, F.; Yang, E.; Kim, D. S.

BACKGROUNDFederal policy now requires sponsors to submit diversity action plans for clinical trials, yet the modifiable features of trial design associated with enrolled composition remain poorly characterized. English-language proficiency requirements are written into eligibility criteria and recorded as free text in a public registry, making them visible and, in principle, amenable to revision, but their relationship to enrolled composition has been examined only within single disease areas and in small samples of trials. We examined whether these requirements and documented language accommodation were associated with the racial and ethnic composition of enrolled participants. METHODS AND FINDINGSWe conducted a cross-sectional study of interventional trials registered on ClinicalTrials.gov with at least one US site and posted results, using a registry snapshot retrieved in October 2026. The primary cohort comprised the 44,487 trials with exclusively US sites, of which 21,924 (49.3%) reported baseline race and 17,434 (39.2%) reported baseline ethnicity with counts that reconciled with the posted denominator. Both exposures were classified from free-text eligibility criteria using regular expressions validated against independent physician review of 150 trials. For the English-language requirement classifier, sensitivity was 88.2% and specificity was 99.2%. The outcome was the trial-level proportion of enrolled participants in each category, analyzed using generalized linear models adjusted for intervention class, therapeutic area, sponsor, phase, enrollment, sites, start year, US Census region, and the population composition of the trials site states. An English-language proficiency requirement appeared in 5,736 trials (12.9%); its prevalence rose from 7.4% of trials starting in 2010 to 26.2% in 2023 (odds ratio [OR] per decade 2.91, P<0.001), whereas documented accommodation, an eligibility statement allowing participation in a language other than English (Spanish in 99.9% of flagged trials), remained rare (788 trials, 1.8%). The requirement was associated with lower enrolled proportions of Asian (OR=0.85, 95% CI 0.77-0.93, P=0.001) and Hispanic/Latinx participants (OR=0.85, 95% CI 0.79-0.91, P<0.001), with no evidence of an association for White participants (OR=0.99, 95% CI 0.95-1.04, P=0.75). Documented accommodation was associated with a higher proportion of Hispanic/Latinx participants (OR=3.23, 95% CI 2.82-3.71, P<0.001). Geographic adjustment did not change the statistical significance of the requirement associations and attenuated the accommodation association. The main limitations were the cross-sectional design, which precludes causal inference; the restriction of enrollment analyses to trials reporting baseline demographics, which were more recent than those without such reporting; and possible misclassification of automatically classified exposures. CONCLUSIONSEnglish-language proficiency requirements became more than three times as common between 2010 and 2023 and were associated with lower enrolled proportions of Asian and Hispanic/Latinx participants. Less common documented language accommodation was associated with a higher enrolled proportion of Hispanic/Latinx participants. Because these criteria are investigator-written and publicly recorded, they may be a tractable target for improving enrollment equity, although whether revising them changes who enrolls requires prospective evaluation.

Personalised normative cardiac phenotyping reveals clinically relevant heterogeneity in cardiac shape and outcome risk

Li, Z.; Bai, W.; Matthews, P. M.; Kainz, B.; Qiao, M.

Standard cardiac biomarkers such as ejection fraction, ventricular volumes, and strain compress complex three-dimensional heart dynamics into a small set of predefined measurements, leaving clinically important heterogeneity unrecognised. Here we propose a personalised normative cardiac frame-work in which each individual is compared to a digital twin representing expected healthy cardiac function given their age, sex, and body habitus. We show that structured patterns of deviation from these normative twins, discovered through systematic exploration of a generative cardiac dynamics space, constitute novel dynamic phenotypes that are not captured by conventional biomarkers. Applied independently to two UK Biobank cardiac cohorts with distinct shape representation, a 128-dimensional four-chamber statistical shape descriptor (HeartSSM, n = 52,365) and a 64-dimensional deep generative latent space from a conditional variational mesh autoencoder (MeshHeart cVAE, n = up to 39,376), we identify reproducible, continuous deviation-from-normative-twin scores (three discrete deviation phenotypes in the HeartSSM cohort) that improve cardiovascular risk stratification beyond ejection fraction, ventricular volumes, myocardial mass, and strain. The strongest effect was seen for incident atrial fibrillation among individuals with preserved ejection fraction ([&ge;]50%): incremental C-index {Delta}C = +0.068 (5-fold cross-validated {Delta}C = +0.063; likelihood-ratio P = 3.3 x 10-8), with a separate, smaller effect for incident stroke in the same preserved-EF subgroup in the independent MeshHeart cVAE representation (which showed no signal for atrial fibrillation) ({Delta}C = +0.024, P = 2.6 x 10-4). Both effects were checked against placebo controls in which the deviation scores were replaced by Gaussian noise. To test whether the normative twin generalises beyond UK Biobanks specific scanning and processing pipeline, we applied the MeshHeart cVAE normative twin, unmodified, to an independent external cohort of healthy volunteers imaged at a different hospital (Gen-Scan/Hammersmith, n = 315): deviation scores in this cohort were of comparable magnitude to the UK Biobank healthy reference (ratio of means = 1.09; Mann-Whitney P = 1.4 x 10-5), indicating that the normative twin captures cross-site-transferable structure rather than a scanner- or pipeline-specific artefact. Our findings suggest that cardiac phenotyping can move beyond predefined measurements toward data-driven dynamic phenotypes that better capture the heterogeneity underlying cardiovascular disease risk, even among individuals whose ejection fraction appears normal.

Decision models can offload structured clinical tasks from large language models

Ariel, D.; Laban, G.; Endelman, S.; Balicer, R.; Dagan, N.; Cohen, S.

Decision models like Jev read text and return probabilities, categories, or ordinal levels, with confidence scores for the latter two, at a fraction of large language models (LLMs) cost. We explored how Jev could be integrated into clinical workflows for all these output types: mortality risk estimation, categorical Choosing-Wisely decisions for patient questions, and ordinal colon cancer staging. With standard LLM-style inputs, Jev trailed five flagship LLMs: mortality AUROC was 0.822 versus 0.837-0.846; Choosing-Wisely accuracy, 87.7% versus 98.4-100%; and staging accuracy, 45.4% versus 94.2-100%. Developer-recommended input alterations improved staging accuracy to 75.8% with medical context, 82.1% with task decomposition, and 85.8% with both. None of Jevs staging errors had full confidence. Using Jev for fully confident staging decisions and LLMs for the rest was non-inferior to each LLM alone and cut flagship LLM costs by 33-44%. Prompted as designed, decision models may efficiently share structured clinical tasks with LLMs.
