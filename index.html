Patient-specific rubric-based evaluation of large language models across longitudinal cancer care

Wei-Wei Zhang, Pengkai Wang, Yan Li, Zhijie Sang, Guan-Qun Zhou, Jun-Yan Li

Large language models (LLMs) show promise in specialty medicine, yet existing evaluations largely focus on isolated clinical encounters rather than tracking how clinical responsibilities evolve across a longitudinal care pathway. Here we introduce NPC-RubricBench, a phase-aware benchmark for evaluating multistage clinical reasoning across the cancer care continuum, using nasopharyngeal carcinoma as a defined disease context. Curated from longitudinal records of 405 patients across two cancer centers, the benchmark comprises 941 patient-phase units across six sequential care phases and 17,688 patient-specific atomic criteria spanning five evaluation dimensions, with each assessment strictly restricted to decision-time clinical evidence. Evaluating 30 general-purpose and medical LLMs revealed that strong diagnostic performance does not carry over to downstream care. We evaluated 30 general-purpose and medical LLMs, showing that high diagnostic performance did not translate into improved downstream care. Instead, the dominant failure modes reversed across phases. Explicit decision errors dominated anatomical staging at 48.2%, whereas decision omissions dominated initial work-up and post-treatment follow-up at 33.4% and 30.4%, respectively. In the evaluated model set, medically adapted models showed higher omission rates than general-purpose models. Moreover, varying query granularity exposes a critical specification trade-off: broad open-ended requests degrade Decision Quality across all 30 models, while more detailed, checklist-like prompts raise Decision Quality but reduce Evidence Faithfulness and Safety Adherence. Furthermore, NPC-RubricBench demonstrates that static diagnostic benchmarks are insufficient to ensure consistent clinical reliability over time, highlighting the importance of phase-adaptive safeguards in multistage medical AI.

A Lightweight Attention-Based CNN with Saliency-Map Interpretability for Skin Cancer Detection

Sanjana Islam Kasfia, Md. Ismiel Hossen Abir, AKM Bahalul Haque, Abir Mahmud Shahariar

Skin cancer is among the most prevalent and life threatening forms of cancer worldwide. Early and accurate diagnosis significantly improves patient survival rates. However, manual dermoscopic assessment remains challenging due to visual similarity. This study proposes an Explainable AI-driven framework for automated binary classification of skin lesions using a lightweight attention-based convolutional neural network (CNN-Attention). The model integrates channel attention to focus on informative regions of dermoscopic images while maintaining low computational complexity. A publicly available dataset comprising 2,637 training and 660 test images was used, with balanced class weights. The proposed model achieved 87 percent accuracy with 1.95 million parameters and 7.45 MB of storage, matching the best baseline (MobileNet) while using approximately half as many parameters and half as much storage. ROC AUC and PR AUC scores reached 0.9475 and 0.9430, respectively. Additionally, saliency maps were incorporated as an explainability technique to visualize regions that influence model decisions. Comparative analysis with VGG16,VGG19,ResNet101,and MobileNet demonstrates the proposed models effectiveness in balancing classification results, computational efficiency, and interpretability.

Enhancing Clinical Classifier Robustness with Deep Representation Learning Based OOD Detection on DNA Methylation Data

Md Zahangir Alom, Quynh T. Tran, Breuer Alexandar, Brent A. Orr

DNA methylation (DNAM) array-based tumor classification is a powerful clinical diagnostic tool, but models trained on fixed reference cohorts can produce faulty predictions for tumor types not represented in the training distribution. We propose an end-to-end deep representation learning-based out-of-distribution (OOD) detection framework for DNAm array data. Deep neural network (DNN) classifiers were trained on public sarcoma (SARC) (GSE140686) and brain tumor (BT) (GSE90496) cohorts, and feature vectors were extracted from bottleneck layers. Five OOD methods, including 1dimension (1D)-Subspace Analysis (1D-SSA), K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), Ridge Regression, Mahalanobis Distance (MD), and Sum of Squared Distances (SSD), were explored with 200 and 500-dimensional embeddings. Spectral discrepancy was used to discriminate in-distribution (IND) from OOD samples. Performance was measured via cross-validation, holdout samples, and an independent clinical dataset. 1D-SSA was the most effective approach. In the sarcoma-IND/brain tumor-OOD setting, the framework achieved an AUROC of 0.975 and True Negative Rate (TNR)@95TPR of 0.930, and an AUROC of 0.9569 and TNR@95TPR of 0.8660 for the brain tumor-IND/sarcoma-OOD setting. On the independent clinical test set, the method yielded a mean AUROC of 0.976, a DE of 0.032, an FPR@95TPR of 0.065, and a TNR@95TPR of 0.935. These results show deep representation-based OOD detection as a practical quality-control layer for clinical-grade methylation-based tumor classification systems.

PathKGen: Pathology Knowledge Assisted MLLM for Explainable Histopathology

Nadun Rajapaksha, Sandun Herath, Devnith Wijesinghe, Isiri Amani Withanawasam, Hasindri Watawana, Ranga Rodrigo

Whole Slide Image (WSI) analysis serves as the fundamental method for the clinical diagnosis of cancer in digital histopathology. Recent advances in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have enabled state-of-the-art (SOTA) automated histopathology report generation. However, MLLMs reason purely through distributions encoded in their neural parameters, limiting the amount of knowledge and reasoning logic they can contain given the model scale. Their black-box reasoning poses another challenge to the human interpretation of intermediate reasoning. We address these issues by utilizing knowledge graphs to inject external structured knowledge for MLLMs to reason over, while making this reasoning component visible. Knowledge graphs are capable of partially externalizing the containment of medical knowledge, an area that remains underexplored in pathology report generation. We introduce PathKGen, a knowledge graph-assisted MLLM for histopathology report generation. PathKGen has two main branches: (1) a visual path that learns visual token inputs to the MLLM using pathology foundation models, and (2) a knowledge path that learns knowledge token inputs by extracting key morphological features of WSIs through querying a pathology-specific knowledge graph. A comprehensive evaluation on WSI-Bench dataset shows that PathKGen surpasses previous SOTA methods while revealing diagnostic reasoning as visually intuitive graphs. Due to its modular design, the proposed knowledge path is complementary to vision-aligned MLLMs and can be extended to other applications by curating domain-specific knowledge graphs to generate knowledge tokens.

Sara Early, Chris Kang, Vibujithan Vigneshwaran, Kimberly Amador, Roger Y. Tsang, Nils D. Forkert

Purpose: Human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) status is a key biomarker relevant for prognostication and treatment decision making in metastatic breast cancer. Thus, the aim of this work was to explore whether HER2 status can be predicted directly from whole-brain post-contrast T1-weighted (T1w) MRI scans in breast cancer brain metastases (BCBM) using deep learning, and whether providing explicit tumour segmentation information improves prediction. Methods: Using the publicly available BCBM-Radiogenomics dataset, we analysed 135 patients, including 70 HER2+ and 65 HER2- patients. Three approaches were implemented and evaluated: a conventional radiomics model, a simple fully convolutional network (SFCN) using T1w MRI scans alone, and an SFCN using T1w MRI scans with an expert-derived tumour mask as an additional input channel. Results: The radiomics model achieved an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC-AUC) of 0.602 (95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.528--0.677) while the SFCN achieved a ROC-AUC of 0.667 (95% CI, 0.63--0.71) using T1w MRI scans alone, which increased to 0.692 (95% CI, 0.58--0.81) when an explicit tumour mask was additionally provided. Conclusion: However, the modest performance across all approaches demonstrates the difficulty of predicting HER2 status from structural MRI scans alone. The difference between the segmentation-free and mask-guided SFCN models suggests that much of the predictive signal captured by the deep-learning model may be learned directly from whole-brain T1w MRI without requiring explicit tumour delineation. These findings support further investigation of segmentation-free deep-learning approaches as a potential non-invasive strategy to support HER2 status determination in BCBM.

These findings highlight the importance of evaluating confounding, predictive reliability, privacy implementation, and deployment efficiency when developing federated AI systems for breast imaging.

These findings position JEV as an initial assessment component that provides criterion-level judgments and flags potential information gaps for review. JEV's observed errors in interpreting clinical evidence underscore the need to verify its outputs against patient records before they inform eligibility decisions.

Analyses of molecules shared across environments support its ability to generate different conformations for the same molecule in different environments.
