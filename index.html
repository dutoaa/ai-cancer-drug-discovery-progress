Comprehensive real-world utilization, treatment trajectories, and outcomes of immune checkpoint inhibitors across cancer types: the multicenter FAIR-ICI cohort

Ha, L. T.; Ver Donck, F.; Bassez, I.; Oeste, C. L.; Vulsteke, C.; Delombaerde, D.; Hens, D.; Prenen, H.; Croes, L.; De Meulenaere, A.; Debruyne, P. R.; Verhaert, M.; Aspeslagh, S.; Brusselaers, N.; Verbiest, A.

BackgroundImmune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have transformed cancer treatment, yet evidence on their utilization, treatment trajectories, and effectiveness in routine clinical practice remains limited. We characterized real-world ICI use and outcomes across cancer types using the multicenter FAIR-ICI cohort and explored differences between real-world and pivotal trial outcomes. MethodsWe conducted a retrospective cohort study of adults receiving ICIs across four Belgian hospitals. Structured and unstructured electronic health record data were harmonized using the Observational Medical Outcomes Partnership Common Data Model (OMOP-CDM) and natural language processing. We evaluated overall survival (OS), time on treatment (ToT), time to next treatment (TTNT), ICI discontinuation (>12-week treatment gap without re-initiation), and post-discontinuation survival. OS was descriptively compared with pivotal trials, and prognostic factors were assessed using multivariable Cox regression. An interactive public dashboard was developed to enable exploration of cohort characteristics, treatment patterns, and outcomes. ResultsAmong 3,231 ICI-treated patients, 2,514 (77.8%) had metastatic disease. Among metastatic patients, 568 (22.6%) discontinued ICI without subsequent systemic therapy. Median post-discontinuation OS was 21.4 months (95% CI 16.5-37.2), with 49.1% alive at 24 months. ToT and TTNT were generally shorter than OS, particularly in melanoma (median OS 78 months; ToT 7 months; TTNT 26 months). Real-world survival was frequently less favorable than in pivotal trials. ECOG [&ge;]2 was associated with poorer OS than ECOG 0-1 (adjusted HR 2.18, 95% CI 1.82-2.60). In NSCLC patients receiving pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy, survival with ECOG 0-1 more closely approximated trial outcomes, whereas survival with ECOG [&ge;]2 was substantially poorer. ConclusionsReal-world ICI treatment trajectories extend beyond active treatment, with some patients experiencing prolonged survival after discontinuation. Differences between real-world and trial outcomes highlight the importance of patient characteristics, particularly performance status, when interpreting trial evidence in routine care. FAIR-ICI provides a scalable framework for evaluating real-world immunotherapy effectiveness, treatment trajectories, and future investigations of treatment-related toxicity. AI-powered single-cell imaging and morphology-guided label-free enrichment reveal tumor heterogeneity, genomic relatedness, and divergence in malignant effusions

Magbanua, M. J. M.; Huppert, L. A.; Cruz, J.; Goudreau, J.; Zhou, S.; Yin, K.; Stejskal, P.; Gill, t.; Carelli, R.; Kim, J.; Hsie, L.; Wang, M.; Lian, Z.; Manepalli, S.; Phelan, M.; Ray, M.; Johnson, C.; Nieto, J.; Schneider, E. C.; Mavropoulos, A.; Delson, A. L.; Simmons, C.; Parizadeh, A.; Crawford, J.; Bruck, M.; Her, L.; Barnett, V.; Cabrera, A. M.; Melisko, M.; Balassanian, R.; Rugo, H. S.; van 't Veer, L.; Jovic, A.; Devine, W. P.; Sayaman, R. W.; Salek, M.; Masaeli, M.; Rosenbluth, J.; Goodarzi, H.

BackgroundMalignant effusions are clinically accessible yet underutilized sources of metastatic tumor cells. However, abundant background immune cells complicate tumor cell isolation and molecular characterization. We integrate AI-powered single-cell imaging and morphology-guided label-free tumor cell enrichment with genomic profiling to characterize phenotypic and genomic heterogeneity in malignant effusions. MethodsWe applied a pre-trained deep learning model to brightfield images of single cells from malignant effusions of 10 patients with metastatic breast cancer to extract morphological features, quantify tumor cell fraction (TCF), and guide label-free microfluidic enrichment. We profiled copy-number alterations and somatic mutations in enriched tumor cell fractions and compared them with available matched effusion cell-free DNA and solid tumors. ResultsAI-powered single-cell morphological analysis identified putative tumor cells in all 10 patients (median TCF, 5.4%; range, 0.51-15.8%; n = 25 samples). AI estimates of major immune-cell populations varied across patients and samples and correlated with flow cytometry measurements (n = 21 paired samples, Pearson r = 0.69-0.75). Unsupervised analysis identified four tumor cell morphotypes, revealing intra- and inter-patient morphological heterogeneity and longitudinal variation in morphotype composition. Genomic profiling of enriched tumor cell fractions by low-pass whole-genome sequencing (n = 26 samples) and whole-exome sequencing (n = 24 samples) identified copy-number alterations and somatic mutations, with tumor fraction influencing genomic signal strength. Comparisons of genomic profiles from tumor cell-enriched fractions with matched effusion cell-free DNA and solid tumors revealed shared genomic alterations, providing orthogonal support for tumor origin and relatedness, while differences across analytes revealed genomic divergence and tumor heterogeneity. ConclusionAI-powered analysis leverages single-cell morphology for tumor cell identification and label-free enrichment, while integration with genomic profiling enables simultaneous interrogation of phenotypic and genomic heterogeneity in malignant effusions. This approach expands the potential of malignant effusions for liquid biopsy applications and biomarker development and discovery.

Enhancing deep sleep with auditory stimulation to boost cognitive training responsiveness in people with Parkinson's disease and mild cognitive impairment: Rationale and design of the double-blind randomized controlled trial PD-CogT-Sleep

Ophey, A.; Kleinsorge, M. T.; Wallimann, N. A.; Maric, A.; Loosli, S. V.; Mollenhauer, B.; Balint, B.; Huber, R.; Baumann, C. R.; Kalbe, E.; Schreiner, S. J.

BackgroundCognitive training (CogT) is an established non-pharmacological intervention for people with Parkinsons disease (PD) and mild cognitive impairment (PD-MCI). However, overall effects on cognitive outcomes are small to moderate and interindividually highly heterogeneous. Sleep -especially deep sleep-could be an important determinant of CogT responsiveness in PD, and may have beneficial effects on PD pathophysiology. Therefore, the enhancement of deep sleep by phase-targeted auditory stimulation (PTAS) could strengthen the effects of CogT on cognitive symptoms and counteract disease progression. MethodsPD-CogT-Sleep is a monocentric, partly decentralized, double-blind, randomized sham-controlled trial that combines two remotely delivered non-pharmacological interventions, to assess the additive effect of PTAS on digital CogT responsiveness in people with PD-MCI. The primary outcome is executive functions; Secondary and exploratory outcomes include further cognitive functions, participant-reported outcome measures, blood-based biomarkers, and high-density sleep electroencephalography markers. Outcomes are assessed at baseline, five weeks post-allocation and at 3-month follow-up. We aim to consecutively recruit N=50 participants, who will be randomized 1:1 to receive CogT+PTAS or CogT+Sham. DiscussionThe PD-CogT-Sleep study integrates wearable and digital technologies to systematically combine a novel, non-invasive, sleep-enhancing intervention (PTAS) with established CogT to improve cognition and evaluate PTAS disease-modifying potential through its effects on blood-based biomarkers in PD-MCI. The expected results will foster our understanding of the interplay of sleep, cognition, and neurodegeneration, and may guide further development of safe, feasible, scalable, patient-centered, technology-based non-pharmacological interventions counteracting cognitive decline in neurodegenerative diseases. AI-Based MRI Radiomics for Preoperative Detection of Tumor Deposits in Rectal Cancer: A Bivariate Diagnostic Accuracy Meta-analysis

Alsaied, M. A.; Hegazy, M.; Saleh, M. M.; Barakat, B.; Ibrahim, O. R.; Abdelrazek, H. M.; Nazemi, S.; El-Hussuna, A.; Shalaby, M.

Background: In rectal cancer (RC), tumor deposits (TDs) are clinically important as they are associated with a negative impact on prognosis and affect risk stratification as well as management plans. MRI radiomics combined with artificial intelligence (AI) has been explored for non-invasive preoperative TDs detection. However, results vary across studies; therefore, we performed a systematic review and meta-analysis to pool their diagnostic test accuracy (DTA). Methods: This systematic review and DTA meta-analysis were conducted in accordance with the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Diagnostic Test Accuracy and reported in adherence to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses of Diagnostic Test Accuracy Studies (PRISMA-DTA). Databases were screened for DTA studies of MRI-based radiomics predicting TDs in adult RC patients with histopathology as reference. Results: Nine studies (2589 patients) were identified. Across included studies, the bivariate meta-analysis showed pooled sensitivity of 0.765 (95% CI 0.714 - 0.809) and specificity of 0.790 (95% CI 0.750 - 0.825), with overall AUC of 0.845 and very low heterogeneity (I2 = 7.3%). In validation datasets, diagnostic performance remained consistent (AUC 0.806), with pooled sensitivity 0.751 (95% CI 0.681 - 0.809) and specificity 0.751 (95% CI 0.681 - 0.809). For MRI modality, T2 weighted image (T2WI) showed higher sensitivity (0.745) than T2WI plus diffusion weighted image (DWI) (0.697), while T2WI+DWI had higher specificity (0.809 vs 0.737) and AUC (0.829 vs 0.796), with no significant subgroup differences (p[&ge;]0.192). Models combining radiomics with clinical/radiological variables achieved significantly higher sensitivity (0.842 vs 0.657, p<0.001) and higher AUC (0.827 vs 0.752), with comparable specificity. Conclusion: MRI-based radiomics demonstrates good accuracy for preoperative detection of tumor deposits in RC. The integration of radiomics with clinical or imaging features increases the accuracy of identification. Amongst the MRI modalities, models that utilize the combination of T2WI plus DWI are more accurate than those using T2WI or DWI only.

Investigating AI's potential in risk-stratifying lung cancer screenings for extending screening intervals and reducing workload: A retrospective analysis of a tertiary hospital cohort

Nowroozi, A.; Bondarenko, M.; Brar, S. S.; Haas, B.; Brown, J.; Rustagi, A.; Graham, B.; Hudson, M.; Su, G.; Yala, A.; Sohn, J. H.

Objective: We aimed to 1) evaluate whether AI algorithms could identify a very low-risk sub-population Lung-RADS 1/2 cases suitable for a safe switch to biennial screening, and 2) externally validate Sybil, an imaging-based deep learning lung cancer risk prediction tool. Materials and methods: We gathered all lung cancer screenings (LCSs) from a tertiary safety-net hospital (n=3120, up to September 2024), manually extracted relevant clinical data, and analyzed Sybil's performance across various subgroups, with a specific focus on Lung-RADS 1 and 2 cases. We simulated LCS interval extension using Sybil and the Brock model risk scores calibrated on a subset of the National Lung Screening Trial (NLST). Confidence intervals were obtained through bootstrapping. Results: The median follow-up was 2.3 years (interquartile range 1.5-3.1), with a total 6-year cancer incidence rate of 2.3% (72/3120). Lung-RADS 1 and 2 cases comprised 90.5% of our scans. Sybil's overall area under the curve (AUC) ranged from 0.819 to 0.912; while its 2-year AUC for Lung-RADS 1 and 2 cases was 0.746. In the screening interval extension simulation, calibrating for a 30% shift to biennial screening translated into 33.8% of negative screens in our data being safely extended using Sybil, with no delayed cancers. The Brock model demonstrated an AUC of 0.513 on our data, with a sensitivity of 18.2% using the 1.5% threshold. Conclusion: Sybil has the potential to safely extend LCS intervals for low-risk individuals, reducing radiology workload and opening spots for life-saving new screenings.
