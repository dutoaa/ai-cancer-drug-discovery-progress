AI-powered single-cell imaging and morphology-guided label-free enrichment reveal tumor heterogeneity, genomic relatedness, and divergence in malignant effusions

Magbanua, M. J. M.; Huppert, L. A.; Cruz, J.; Goudreau, J.; Zhou, S.; Yin, K.; Stejskal, P.; Gill, t.; Carelli, R.; Kim, J.; Hsie, L.; Wang, M.; Lian, Z.; Manepalli, S.; Phelan, M.; Ray, M.; Johnson, C.; Nieto, J.; Schneider, E. C.; Mavropoulos, A.; Delson, A. L.; Simmons, C.; Parizadeh, A.; Crawford, J.; Bruck, M.; Her, L.; Barnett, V.; Cabrera, A. M.; Melisko, M.; Balassanian, R.; Rugo, H. S.; van 't Veer, L.; Jovic, A.; Devine, W. P.; Sayaman, R. W.; Salek, M.; Masaeli, M.; Rosenbluth, J.; Goodarzi, H.

BackgroundMalignant effusions are clinically accessible yet underutilized sources of metastatic tumor cells. However, abundant background immune cells complicate tumor cell isolation and molecular characterization. We integrate AI-powered single-cell imaging and morphology-guided label-free tumor cell enrichment with genomic profiling to characterize phenotypic and genomic heterogeneity in malignant effusions. MethodsWe applied a pre-trained deep learning model to brightfield images of single cells from malignant effusions of 10 patients with metastatic breast cancer to extract morphological features, quantify tumor cell fraction (TCF), and guide label-free microfluidic enrichment. We profiled copy-number alterations and somatic mutations in enriched tumor cell fractions and compared them with available matched effusion cell-free DNA and solid tumors. ResultsAI-powered single-cell morphological analysis identified putative tumor cells in all 10 patients (median TCF, 5.4%; range, 0.51-15.8%; n = 25 samples). AI estimates of major immune-cell populations varied across patients and samples and correlated with flow cytometry measurements (n = 21 paired samples, Pearson r = 0.69-0.75). Unsupervised analysis identified four tumor cell morphotypes, revealing intra- and inter-patient morphological heterogeneity and longitudinal variation in morphotype composition. Genomic profiling of enriched tumor cell fractions by low-pass whole-genome sequencing (n = 26 samples) and whole-exome sequencing (n = 24 samples) identified copy-number alterations and somatic mutations, with tumor fraction influencing genomic signal strength. Comparisons of genomic profiles from tumor cell-enriched fractions with matched effusion cell-free DNA and solid tumors revealed shared genomic alterations, providing orthogonal support for tumor origin and relatedness, while differences across analytes revealed genomic divergence and tumor heterogeneity. ConclusionAI-powered analysis leverages single-cell morphology for tumor cell identification and label-free enrichment, while integration with genomic profiling enables simultaneous interrogation of phenotypic and genomic heterogeneity in malignant effusions. This approach expands the potential of malignant effusions for liquid biopsy applications and biomarker development and discovery.

Suvorexant Inversely Affects Phosphorylated Tau in Human Cerebrospinal Fluid and Plasma

Pedersen, T. J.; Yu, J.; Li, A.; Ibanez, L.; Bubu, O. M.; Lucey, B. P.

Alzheimer disease (AD) is characterized by amyloid-{beta} plaques and neurofibrillary tangles of tau. Suvorexant, a dual orexin receptor antagonist, was previously shown to acutely reduce the ratio of phosphorylated tau-181 to unphosphorylated tau-181 (pT181/T181) in cerebrospinal fluid (CSF). However, its effects on plasma pT181/T181 and broader proteomic networks remain unknown. Here, we conducted a secondary analysis of samples from a randomized, placebo-controlled trial of 25 participants receiving placebo or suvorexant 20 mg. Paired CSF and plasma samples were collected longitudinally via indwelling intrathecal and intravenous catheters. Plasma AD biomarkers were quantified by mass spectrometry, and proteomic profiling was performed using the SomaScan 11K assay on samples from hours 0, 16, and 24. Suvorexant increased pT181/T181 in plasma while decreasing it in CSF between 08:00-16:00. At the pathway level, suvorexant increased the extracellular abundance of proteins associated with translation, RNA binding, cytoskeletal organization and endosomal sorting, centrally at 16 hours and peripherally at 24 hours, and decreased cytokine and receptor-ligand signaling and serine protease activity in plasma at 24 hours. As plasma biomarkers are increasingly used for clinical screening and trial enrollment, factors that influence their levels, including medications, must be identified and their effects on interpretation understood.

Phenome-wide and proteomic analysis of APOE alleles across different ancestries in the All of Us Research Program

Mejia Garcia, A.; Valderrama-Carmona, P.; Zheng, T. M.; Wang, Z.; Tsao, H. M.; Jung, J.; Orozco, C. A.; Su, C.-Y.; Zhou, S.

BackgroundApolipoprotein E (APOE) {varepsilon}4 is the strongest common genetic risk factor for Alzheimers disease and a determinant of cardiometabolic risk, yet studies of APOE genotypes with disease associations and plasma proteomic signatures have been primarily restricted to European-ancestry cohorts. MethodsWe performed a phenome-wide association study of APOE genotype in 367,757 All of Us participants across six genetic ancestry groups. APOE alleles were tested against 2,632 phenotypes using an ordinal Alzheimers-disease-risk hierarchy ({varepsilon}2/{varepsilon}2 < {varepsilon}2/{varepsilon}3 < {varepsilon}3/{varepsilon}3 < {varepsilon}2/{varepsilon}4 < {varepsilon}3/{varepsilon}4 < {varepsilon}4/{varepsilon}4), adjusted for age, sex at birth, EHR length, and 16 genetic principal components. Sex- and ancestry-stratified analyses used Cochrans Q for heterogeneity. We evaluated {varepsilon}2 and {varepsilon}4 dosage effects on plasma protein levels in 9,132 participants across five ancestries. FindingsWe identified 27 phenotypes associated with APOE, mainly in neurological and lipid-related traits. Five lipid-related traits showed sex heterogeneity, with larger effects in females. Nine associations showed ancestry-specific effects. APOE {varepsilon}4 showed stronger protection against fatty liver disease in East Asians with lack of protection in African ancestry; and larger dementia risk for Europeans. Finally, SNAP25 was identified as positively associated with APOE {varepsilon}4 dosage across ancestries, and CDC42EP1, APOA1 and ITGB5 shown associations with APOE {varepsilon}2/{varepsilon}4 specifically in non-European populations. InterpretationAncestry-heterogeneous APOE effects, particularly for liver and dementia phenotypes, argue for ancestry-aware interpretation of APOE-based risk. Plasma SNAP25 is a candidate ancestry-portable biomarker of APOE-{varepsilon}4 effects, and ancestry-specific proteins nominate mechanisms for the observed phenotypic heterogeneity. Research in context Evidence before this studyThe APOE {varepsilon}4 variant is an established genetic risk factor for hyperlipidemia, Alzheimers disease, dementia, ischemic heart disease and, more recently, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. Prior phenome-wide association studies (PheWAS) of APOE have been conducted largely in European-ancestry cohorts. A recent APOE PheWAS in the All of Us Research Program (n = 181,880) identified 17 Bonferroni-significant phenotypes and preliminary evidence of ancestry heterogeneity for hyperlipidemia and mild cognitive impairment, but statistical power in non-European groups limited systematic characterization of ancestry-specific effects. Recent plasma proteomic studies have described APOE-associated protein signatures shared with neurodegenerative diseases, but these were confined to European-ancestry participants, leaving open whether such signatures generalize across ancestries. Added value of this studyUsing the All of Us v9 release, we approximately doubled the sample size of the prior APOE PheWAS in this cohort (n = 367,757 across six ancestry groups, including [~]146,000 non-European participants), enabling the first well-powered assessment of ancestry heterogeneity of APOE effects. We identify 27 Bonferroni-significant phecodes, 11 novel compared to the previous APOE PheWAS in All of Us -- including vascular dementia, dementia with Lewy bodies, and angina pectoris. We detect significant sex heterogeneity for five lipid-related phecodes and significant ancestry heterogeneity for nine phecodes, including stronger protection against fatty liver and chronic liver disease in East Asian participants and larger dementia risk in European participants. Complementing the phenotypic analysis, we present the first ancestry-stratified plasma proteomic characterization of APOE genotypes (n = 9,132), identifying SNAP25 as a robust APOE-{varepsilon}4-associated protein across ancestries and ancestry-specific signals for IL32, CDC42EP1, APOA1, and ITGB5. Implications of all the available evidenceAPOE-associated risk estimates derived predominantly from European-ancestry data may not transfer directly to other populations, and our findings support incorporating ancestry into the interpretation and calibration of APOE-based risk prediction. The ancestry-heterogeneous protection against liver disease and the parallel APOA1 signal absent in East and South Asian participants together offer a mechanistic hypothesis linking APOE-driven lipid partitioning to hepatic outcomes. Plasma SNAP25 is proposed as an accessible, ancestry-portable biomarker of APOE-{varepsilon}4 effects and Alzheimers disease progression. Ancestry-specific proteins (IL32 and CDC42EP1 in African ancestry, ITGB5 in Admixed American ancestry) constitute potential drivers of ancestry heterogeneity in APOE-related disease risk.

Materials and methodsWe gathered all lung cancer screenings (LCSs) from a tertiary safety-net hospital (n=3120, up to September 2024), manually extracted relevant clinical data, and analyzed Sybils performance across various subgroups, with a specific focus on Lung-RADS 1 and 2 cases. ObjectiveAccurate lateralization of the epileptogenic focus is essential for presurgical evaluation of patients with drug-resistant epilepsy. This study examined whether diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) measures of white matter microstructure, combined with clinical and demographic variables, could classify epilepsy lateralization using machine learning. MethodsWe present a retrospective analysis of 334 adult patients undergoing presurgical epilepsy evaluation at the University of Kansas Health System. Fractional anisotropy (FA) and mean diffusivity (MD) were extracted from 36 bilateral white matter tracts and combined with clinical and demographic variables. Several supervised machine learning approaches were evaluated, with elastic net logistic regression selected for detailed analysis. Discrimination between unilateral epilepsy and bilateral or generalized epilepsy was also good (AUC = 0.83). Classification of right-sided versus left-sided epilepsy demonstrated lower performance (AUC = 0.76). Physiological Waveform Features Based Aging Clocks Associate with Cardiovascular Disease

Pal, R.; Barney, A.; Cannesson, M.

Aging affects the morphology of photoplethysmography (PPG) and arterial blood pressure (ABP) waveforms. Here, we demonstrate that machine learning (ML) models trained on features derived from PPG or ABP waveforms can predict an individual's age. We further investigated whether the discrepancy between predicted and chronological age (Age-Gap) could serve as a measure of cardiovascular health by testing its association with cardiovascular disease (CVD). From a large perioperative dataset of 17,327 surgical patients, high-fidelity PPG and ABP waveforms were analyzed, and over 840 time-, frequency-, and statistical-domain features per cardiac cycle were extracted using a signal-processing-based tool. These features were then used to develop ML-based aging clocks, PPGAge and ABPAge, trained on PPG and ABP features, respectively. For each aging clock, we evaluated five ML models: Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM), eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), Random Forest, Lasso, and Elastic Net. Individuals were classified as having accelerated, normal, or delayed aging based on their Age-Gap relative to the model-specific mean absolute error (MAE, rounded up to the nearest whole year): accelerated aging was defined as Age-Gap [&ge;] +MAE, normal aging as -MAE [&le;] Age-Gap < +MAE, and delayed aging as Age-Gap < -MAE. LightGBM provided the best age predictions for both aging clocks, with Pearson correlation coefficients (r) of 0.92 and 0.92 and R-squared values of 0.81 and 0.82 for PPGAge and ABPAge, respectively. Moreover, we found that accelerated aging was significantly associated with higher odds of CVD for both aging clocks, PPGAge (odds ratio, 1.87 [95% CI, 1.59-2.21], p<0.001) and ABPAge (odds ratio, 2.28 [95% CI, 1.51-3.43], p<0.001). Additionally, delayed aging was significantly associated with lower odds of CVD for PPGAge (odds ratio, 0.68 [95% CI, 0.58-0.80], p<0.001). Our findings suggest that aging clocks based on high-fidelity PPG or ABP waveform features may serve as novel biomarkers for identifying individuals with higher odds of CVD.

Alzheimers disease and psychiatric polygenic risk are disease-specific and independent of plasma biomarkers in a real-world young-onset dementia cohort.

Bhalala, O. G.; Wang, L.; Gerring, Z. F.; Han, J.; Kang, M. J.; Trounson, B. O.; Loi, S. M.; Velakoulis, D.; Eratne, D.; Bahlo, M.; The MiND Study Group,

BackgroundYoung-onset dementia is frequently misdiagnosed, particularly where neurodegenerative and primary psychiatric disorders (PPD) overlap. Plasma biomarkers measure current pathology, whereas polygenic risk scores (PRS) capture lifelong genetic risk. Whether PRS derived from late-onset and population samples transfer to young-onset presentations, and how they relate to plasma biomarkers, is unknown. MethodsWe studied 687 participants of European ancestry from the Markers in Neuropsychiatric Disorders (MiND) Study, a prospective young-onset neuropsychiatry cohort: 195 healthy controls (HC), 283 PPD, 55 Alzheimers disease (AD) and 154 non-AD neurodegenerative disease (oNDG). PRS for AD, a cross-disorder psychiatric factor (PSY), frontotemporal dementia, Lewy body dementia and Parkinsons disease were computed with PRS-CS, excluding the APOE locus. Plasma NfL, GFAP and p-tau217 were available for 332 participants. Models were adjusted for age, sex and APOE {varepsilon}4 dose. ResultsRelative to HC, the AD PRS was associated with AD (OR 1.69 per SD, 95% CI 1.17-2.45) but not PPD (0.95, 0.77-1.16). The PSY PRS showed the reciprocal pattern (PPD 1.29, 1.05-1.58; AD 1.00, 0.72-1.40). No PRS was associated with any plasma protein. In distinguishing PPD from oNDG, NfL was the strongest discriminator (OR 2.15, 1.62-2.83). The AD PRS was also associated with oNDG (1.38, 1.11-1.71), without improving discrimination (AUC 0.764 for NfL alone versus 0.773 combined). ConclusionIn young-onset presentations, AD and psychiatric polygenic risk are disease-specific and independent of plasma biomarkers of neurodegeneration. Their combined diagnostic value requires evaluation in larger, longitudinal pre-clinical cohorts.

CSF PrP-amyloid seeding predicts clinical onset in inherited prion disease

Mok, T. H.; Nihat, A.; Vallabh, S. M.; Noa, B.; Omer, N.; Geschwind, M.; Arnold, S. E.; Minikel, E. V.; Collinge, J.; Mead, S.

Silent proteopathic seed propagation is hypothesised to precede neurodegenerative disease, but direct evidence that it signals near-term clinical conversion in humans is lacking. In prion disease, a prototypic disorder of proteopathic seeding, cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) real-time quaking-induced conversion (RT-QuIC) offers a direct measure of prion protein amyloid seeding activity. We analysed longitudinal CSF RT-QuIC data from 94 PRNP E200K mutation carriers across international natural history cohorts to determine whether seeding status predicts clinical conversion. Eleven carriers developed inherited prion disease; all showed at least one positive RT-QuIC result before onset. First positivity appeared a median of 2.0 years prior to symptoms (IQR 1.3-3.3; range 0.2-4.8). Landmark analyses showed RT-QuIC positivity identified a markedly increased short-term conversion risk, whereas persistently asymptomatic carriers remained negative throughout follow-up. These findings establish CSF RT-QuIC positivity as a biomarker-defined state of incipient conversion in E200K carriers, informing risk stratification and preventive trial design.
