Baseline Prognosis and Treatment Benefit From First-Line Immunotherapy Strategies: Target Trial Emulations Across Advanced Solid Tumors

Orcutt, X.; Nimgaonkar, V.; Chen, K.; Riaz, I.; Cohen, A.; Mamtani, R.; Gomes, M.; Long, Q.; Parikh, R. B.

BackgroundIn randomized trials, first-line immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)-based therapy has shown excess early mortality versus chemotherapy- or targeted therapy-containing regimens. ICI monotherapy is often chosen for frail patients because it is perceived as less toxic. Whether treatment benefit varies with baseline prognosis is unknown, as poor-prognosis patients are underrepresented in trials. MethodsUsing the US Flatiron Health electronic health record-derived database, we emulated target trials among 8,688 patients initiating first-line therapy during 2011-2023 across five comparisons: pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy versus pembrolizumab in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC; PD-L1 CPS [&ge;]1 or unknown) and non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC; PD-L1 TPS [&ge;]50%); carboplatin plus gemcitabine versus pembrolizumab in urothelial carcinoma; pembrolizumab versus chemotherapy in dMMR/MSI-H colorectal cancer; and ipilimumab plus nivolumab versus BRAF/MEK inhibition in BRAF-mutant melanoma. Baseline prognosis was estimated with machine learning models predicting 6-month survival. Overlap-weighted pseudo-observation regression evaluated whether differences in 2-year restricted mean survival time (RMST) varied with predicted survival. ResultsIn HNSCC, adding chemotherapy to pembrolizumab did not clearly improve RMST overall (11.5 days; 95% CI, -18.4 to 41.1), but benefit was 102.4 days greater at the 10th versus 90th percentile of predicted survival (95% CI, 40.5 to 164.3; interaction P=.001). Similar gradients favored carboplatin plus gemcitabine over pembrolizumab in urothelial carcinoma (10th-90th percentile difference, 51.4 days; 95% CI, 4.8 to 98.1; P=.03) and, less certainly, adding chemotherapy to pembrolizumab in NSCLC (73.7 days; 95% CI, -3.7 to 151.2; P=.06). Higher-risk patients in these cohorts were more often trial-ineligible. ICI-based therapy improved overall RMST in colorectal cancer (difference, 79.7 days; 95% CI, 49.5 to 107.0) and melanoma (68.3 days; 95% CI, 33.9 to 101.9), without clear variation by predicted survival (interaction P=.32 and P=.91). ConclusionsAmong patients with poorer prognosis, chemotherapy-containing regimens were associated with greater benefit than ICI monotherapy in HNSCC and urothelial carcinoma, and possibly NSCLC. ICI-based therapy appeared beneficial across prognosis in dMMR/MSI-H colorectal cancer and BRAF-mutant melanoma. Baseline prognosis may complement tumor biomarkers when selecting first-line immunotherapy strategies.

WITHDRAWN: Predicting Distant Melanoma Metastasis at Diagnosis Using Machine Learning

Kim, J. J. H.; Lee, J. W. Y.; Yuan, H.; Han, C.; Zandigohar, M.; Haber, R.; Tsoukas, M.; Avanaki, K.

Distant melanoma metastasis at the time of diagnosis is uncommon, but has major implications for patient prognosis and treatment selection. However, few tools can reliably predict the risk of distant metastasis at initial presentation. Here, we developed and evaluated machine learning models to predict distant melanoma metastasis using routinely captured clinicopathologic and demographic variables across all histologic subtypes. Using the National Cancer Institute Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) program from 2010-2022, we identified adults aged 20 to 90 years with melanoma as the first and only primary malignancy (n=51,285). Explainable Boosting Machine achieved a strong balance of discrimination and precision (AUROC = 0.947, AUPRC = 0.610, Precision = 0.793, Brier = 0.015). At 90% sensitivity, specificity was 0.843 with consistent performance across cross-validation folds. Clinicopathologic variables, including T stage, Breslow thickness, ulceration, and mitotic activity, contributed the largest share of predictive signal across descriptive, regression-based, and SHAP analyses, with smaller contributions from demographic factors. Decision curve analysis supported clinical utility, showing a net reduction of 88.3 per 100 patients and a standardized net benefit of 0.541. This model could be used to identify patients at sufficiently elevated risk to justify staging PET/CT despite otherwise localized clinical presentation. Cost-consequence analysis further showed that imaging true- and false-positive patients at 85% to 95% sensitivity threshold nearly doubled downstream imaging cost. We deployed the final model as an online calculator to support exploration of individualized risk estimates (https://melanoma-calculator.streamlit.app/).

CB-COMIL: interpretable prediction of melanoma immunotherapyresponse from multiplex immunofluorescence whole-slide images

Rana, P.; Gide, T. N.; Adegoke, N.; Mao, Y.; N. Lo, S.; Berkovsky, S.; Coiera, E.; Long, G. V.; Wilmott, J. S.; Liu, S.

Deep learning models can predict melanoma immunotherapy response but rarely explain predictions biologically. Concept bottleneck models offer interpretable predictions, but have not been applied to multiplex immunofluorescence (mIF), and whether their learned concepts reproduce tissue measurements remains unclear. We developed CB-COMIL, which routes mIF predictions through four supervised concepts (immune:tumour ratio, CD8/SOX10 balance, CD68 relative signal and PD-L1 evasion index), decomposing each prediction exactly into concept contributions. Developed on 211 slides and evaluated on 42 slides from an independent, differently acquired test cohort, CB-COMIL achieved macro-F1 0.69 versus 0.67 for an opaque comparator (difference 0.020, 95% CI -0.103 to 0.144). In 41 slides with complete measurements, learned concepts correlated with measured counterparts (Spearman{rho} 0.61-0.76), yet substituting measured values reduced macro-F1 from 0.68 to 0.48. CB-COMIL makes predictions interpretable without detectable performance loss, and our evaluation shows that concepts can faithfully determine predictions without reproducing the corresponding biological measurements.

Plasma DNA fragmentomic analysis for evaluation of patients with pancreatic cystic neoplasms

Marcinak, C. T.; Stephens, M. D.; Chen, J.; Merali, N.; McDonald, B. R.; Dietmann, E. C.; Nawaz, M. T.; McGregor, S. M.; Ronnekleiv-Kelly, S. M.; Weber, S. M.; Nkadori, E. N.; Stadmeyer, P. G.; Benson, M. E.; Gopal, D. V.; Zafar, S. N.; Kelly, K. J.; Mathew, J.; Pirasteh, A.; Frampton, A. E.; Pfau, P. R.; Sivakumar, S.; Minter, R. M.; Murtaza, M.

BackgroundIncidental detection of pancreatic cystic neoplasms (PCNs) is common, but to identify patients with malignant PCNs (high-grade dysplasia or carcinoma) who need invasive testing such as endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) or surgery is challenging. Plasma DNA fragmentomic analysis has recently shown promise for early detection of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC). ObjectiveTo evaluate the ability of plasma DNA fragmentomic analysis to differentiate between patients with benign and malignant PCNs. DesignWe performed whole genome sequencing of pre-treatment plasma DNA from 121 patients with PDAC, 121 age and sex-matched healthy controls, and 99 patients with PCNs referred for EUS (total n=341). After training and validating a plasma DNA fragmentomics machine learning model for PDAC detection, we independently evaluated its performance for detection of malignant PCNs, and compared with circulating glycoproteins. ResultsEleven of 99 patients (11%) were diagnosed with malignant PCNs during 24.6 months of clinical follow-up. For PDAC detection, plasma DNA analysis showed area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) of 0.97. For malignant PCN detection, plasma DNA analysis showed AUROC of 0.67, while CA19-9, CA125 and CEA showed AUROCs of 0.78, 0.54, and 0.63, respectively. Combined analysis of plasma DNA with glycoproteins showed AUROC of 0.92, demonstrating 91% sensitivity, 99% negative predictive value, 88% specificity and 48% positive predictive value for malignant PCN detection. ConclusionPlasma DNA fragmentomics and glycoprotein biomarkers showed complementary performance for detecting malignant PCNs in patients referred for EUS. This approach could improve patient selection for EUS, and warrants further evaluation.

Mechanistic Assessment of Norepinephrine Therapy versus Angiotensin-II on Organ Function in Septic Shock (MANTRA): Study Protocol for a multicenter open-label randomized trial

Filbin, M.; Leisman, D. E.; Pachano-Bravo, A.; Goldberg, M. B.; Hibbert, K. A.; Nelson, O.; Rappaport, B.; Mahler, S. A.; Harris, L.; Besong, E. B.; Reeves, B.; Maves, R. C.; Petrilli, A.; Schaich, C. L.; Files, D. C.; Gibbs, K.; Chappell, M. C.; Khanna, A. K.

IntroductionNorepinephrine remains first-line vasopressor therapy for septic shock despite its association at high doses with undesirable cardiovascular, kidney, and immune effects. Angiotensin-II (Ang-II) is a non-catecholamine vasopressor of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) now approved to increase blood pressure in vasodilatory shock. RAAS dysregulation is common in septic shock and may represent a treatable trait. However, few prospective investigations evaluate the effect of early Ang-II use in septic shock on dysfunctional RAAS biology, host-response, or clinical outcomes. Therefore, we designed a mechanistic clinical trial to test the hypothesis that early Ang-II therapy in septic shock normalizes systemic RAAS abnormalities and modulates innate immune responses. Methods and AnalysisThe Mechanistic Assessment of Norepinephrine TheRapy vs. Angiotensin-II in Septic Shock (MANTRA) trial is an investigator-initiated, prospective, multicenter, open-label, randomized mechanistic clinical trial. Adults with septic shock by Sepsis-3 criteria receiving a norepinephrine-equivalent dose (NED) of 0.10-0.50 mcg/kg/min will be randomized 1:1 to primary Ang-II (to a maximum 40 ng/kg/min) or norepinephrine (to a maximum 35 mcg/min) treatment strategies over a 48-hour period. The primary outcome is the between-group difference in plasma renin trajectory from baseline to 24 hours represented as a treatment-by-time interaction term in a linear mixed-effects model. With n=78, the trial has 90% power (at two-sided =0.05) to detect a 25% between-group difference in 24-hour renin. Secondary clinical outcomes include longitudinal NED, hours alive and vasopressor-free at 72 hours, a 28-day composite outcome (days alive and free of vasopressors, renal replacement therapy, and mechanical ventilation), treatment failure requiring crossover or protocol discontinuation, and thrombotic and arrhythmia events through 28 days. Secondary mechanistic outcomes include serial RAAS biomarkers (prorenin, soluble prorenin receptor, angiotensinogen, ACE, ACE2, Ang-II, angiotensin-(1-7), aldosterone, and DPP3), immune measures (ex vivo monocyte function, monocyte gene expression, plasma cytokines), and organ injury biomarkers. Enrollment is planned from 11/2026-7/2030. Ethics and DisseminationThe study has been approved by the Advocate Health/Wake Forest University School of Medicine ethics commitee (IRB00115287). Informed consent is required. Results will be submitted for publication in peer-reviewed journals and presented at national and international scientific meetings, regardless of the direction of findings. Interpretable competing-risk prediction of hepatocellular carcinoma in cirrhosis from region-wide routine health data

Gutierrez-Alvarez, I.; Rojas, A.; Calzadilla-Bertot, L.; Dopazo, J.; Romero-Gomez, M.; Loucera, C.

BackgroundShort-term prediction of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) in cirrhosis is complicated by the competing risk of death from liver disease. We aimed to develop an interpretable model on region-wide routine data that predicts HCC while separating competing mortality, and to compare it with established risk scores. MethodsFrom a region-wide cohort of adults with cirrhosis in the Andalusian Population Health Database, we trained the model on 17,177 patients with a prediction date set 12 months before their outcome, between 2020 and 2023. From one year of electronic health records before that date (demographics, liver and chronic disease codes, and eight routine laboratory tests), an Explainable Boosting Machine without interaction terms classified the following 12 months as HCC, death or liver transplantation without HCC, or event-free. We evaluated it in 20 repeats of 5-fold cross-validation against a binary model, a model with pair interactions and a model using the full health record, all on the same folds. We then applied the cross-validated models, without retraining, at the first routine encounter at which aMAP or the Toronto HCC Risk Index could be computed, and compared them with the scores in patients followed forward under competing risks, with and without a 6-month washout and with each province left out of training in turn. Findings771 patients (4.5%) developed HCC within 12 months and 3,314 (19.3%) died or underwent liver transplantation without HCC (3,061 deaths, 253 transplants). In cross-validation, the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) for HCC was 0.734 (SD 0.018). A binary formulation, pairwise interactions and the full health record (more than 3,000 features) did not improve it; the full record lowered it by 0.024. At routine encounters the model discriminated 12-month HCC better than aMAP (time-dependent AUC 0.790 vs 0.688; difference +0.102, 95% CI +0.071 to +0.132) and the Toronto HCC Risk Index (0.761 vs 0.703; +0.057, +0.032 to +0.081), and remained better after excluding HCC diagnosed within 6 months (concordance difference +0.055 and +0.040) and when each of the eight provinces was left out of training. At equal group size, its high-risk group held as much HCC as the scores high-risk categories, with 20-28% fewer competing deaths. Its shape functions separated cancer-specific signal (AFP rising from 2 to 20 {micro}g/L, platelets below 150 x109/L) from liver failure (bilirubin above 51 {micro}mol/L, albumin below 35 g/L); a binary model showed only the first. InterpretationAn interpretable three-class model on routine data outperformed established scores for short-term HCC risk in cirrhosis, while showing whether each abnormal value points to cancer or to competing death. FundingThis work was supported by grants PID2023_152380OB and PRTR-C17.I1 from the Spanish Ministry of Science and Innovation; grant PMP24/00005 from the Instituto de Salud Carlos III (ISCIII); grant IE19_259 FPS from Consejeria de Salud y Consumo, Junta de Andalucia; and Horizon Europe (HORIZON), Marie Skodowska-Curie Actions Doctoral Networks (101226456-MLCARE). The funders had no role in study design, data collection, data analysis, data interpretation or writing of the report. Research in contextO_ST_ABSEvidence before this studyC_ST_ABSWe searched PubMed to October 2026 for HCC risk prediction in cirrhosis with machine learning or risk scores. Machine-learning models usually outperform aMAP and the Toronto HCC Risk Index (THRI) in development data. They are mostly specific to one etiology (HBV or HCV), built on inputs not routinely available (elastography), black boxes explained post hoc, and evaluated without competing risks. A meta-analysis of models for non-viral liver disease found only one developed with a competing-risk approach, and rated all 14 included studies at high risk of bias (1). In prospective French cohorts analysed under competing risks, machine learning did not outperform aMAP (2). Added value of this studyIn a region-wide population of 17,177 adults with cirrhosis of all etiologies, we developed an interpretable three-class model on routine records: HCC, death or transplant, or event-free. Under competing risks, at the first encounter where each score could be computed, it ranked HCC better than aMAP and THRI, including after a 6-month washout. At the same group size it flagged as much HCC with 20-28% fewer patients who died first. The advantage held when each province was left out of training. Its shape functions are the whole model, not a post-hoc approximation, and separate markers of cancer from markers of liver failure. A binary model on the same data ranks HCC as well but folds the death signal into its single curve, as we show analytically. Implications of all the available evidenceSurveillance programmes can rank patients from data they already hold, with a model whose every contribution can be inspected. Modelling death as an outcome keeps patients unlikely to benefit off the high-risk list. External validation in other health systems, with local recalibration, is the next step.

Spatially localized racial differences in the association between white matter hyperintensities and verbal memory in cognitively unimpaired adults

Wu, J.; Lin, Q.; James, T. A.; Levey, A. I.; Lah, J. J.; Qiu, D.; The HABS-HD Study Team,

White matter hyperintensities (WMH) are common in aging, but whether their cognitive associations differ between African American and White older adults is unclear. We studied 5,187 cognitively unimpaired non-Hispanic African American (NHA) and non-Hispanic White (NHW) older adults across three independent cohorts using depth- and lobe-resolved WMH mapping with cross-sectional, longitudinal and machine learning analyses. In the Emory Healthy Brain Study (EHBS), greater WMH burden was more strongly associated with poorer verbal memory among NHA than NHW participants. A similar difference was observed in a 34-center cohort but not detected in the third cohort. Differences localized to frontal juxtacortical and parietal deep white matter in EHBS and the multicenter cohort, respectively, and did not parallel WMH burden distributions. Estimates changed little after adjustment for cohort-specific Alzheimers disease (AD) biomarkers, vascular risk factors, structural imaging measures and APOE genotype. Accounting for neighborhood deprivation in EHBS yielded a similar estimate. Generalized random forests supported these differences without assuming a linear interaction. Baseline frontal juxtacortical WMH burden showed a stronger association with subsequent AD-signature cortical thinning among NHA than NHW participants in EHBS, with directionally similar multicenter findings. Spatially resolved WMH measures may help identify early heterogeneity in vascular contributions to cognitive aging.
