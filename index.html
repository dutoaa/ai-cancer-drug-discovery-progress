LungAI: An AI Biomarker that Refines Lung-RADS v2022 Triage of Screen-Detected Pulmonary Nodules

Suri, H.; Keselman, Y.; Legel, L.; Govette, J.; Mullen, B.; Choradia, N.; Sartawi, T.; Abtin, F.; Moghanaki, D.; Thomson, C. C.; Yankelevitz, D. F.; Ahuja, Y.

BackgroundThe Lung-RADS algorithm standardizes the management of screen-detected pulmonary nodules, but its risk assessment is driven chiefly by nodule size and attenuation. This misallocates a meaningful fraction of patients: a nontrivial share of small malignancies receive 12-month surveillance while large benign nodules proceed to invasive workup. Artificial intelligence (AI) may supply risk information independent of the imaging features Lung-RADS already uses. MethodsWe validated LungAI, an investigational deep learning nodule malignancy classifier not yet cleared by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA), on the held-out NLST test partition of Ardila et al. (2,747 low-dose CT examinations from 1,244 patients with [&ge;]1 solid or part-solid 4-30 mm nodule; 89 pathologically confirmed cancers, prevalence 3.2%). We compared discrimination against Sybil, mean nodule diameter, and estimated nodule volume. A board-certified radiologist assigned Lung-RADS v2022 categories to a positives-enriched random subset (226 eligible examinations; 78 cancers), projected to the full cohort by contingency weighting. A per-category reclassification algorithm shifted patients along the existing Lung-RADS management ladder using a-priori score cutpoints. We evaluated a separately locked follow-up configuration on examinations with a prior scan. ResultsLungAI achieved AUROC 0.92 (95% CI 0.88-0.95) and AUPRC 0.47 (0.35-0.61), outperforming Sybil ({Delta}AUROC 0.07; {Delta}AUPRC 0.18), diameter ({Delta}AUROC 0.14; {Delta}AUPRC 0.34), and volume ({Delta}AUROC 0.14; {Delta}AUPRC 0.38; all P<0.001). Against Lung-RADS v2022 scored ordinally on the weighted annotated subset, LungAI improved AUPRC ({Delta}AUPRC 0.31, P=0.003) but not AUROC ({Delta}AUROC 0.03, P=0.20). LungAI added discriminative information independent of diameter (likelihood-ratio {chi}2=161, P<10-36; {Delta}AUROC 0.10) and, on follow-up examinations, independent of delta diameter, volume doubling time, and new-nodule status ({chi}2=79, P<10-18). Integrated with Lung-RADS, LungAI reduced cancers on the slowest (12-month rescan) pathway by 46% (3.1[-&gt;]1.6 per 1,000 examinations) and benign biopsy referrals by 20% (32.7[-&gt;]26.2 per 1,000 examinations) at approximately unchanged total biopsy volume (51.0[-&gt;]50.1 per 1,000). Assuming full adherence to recommended follow-up, the combined approach yielded 25 months of net diagnostic lead time, an estimated net cost saving of [~]$2,200, and 6.5 fewer benign biopsies per 1,000 examinations; accounting for published adherence rates, it yielded 1.6 additional cancer diagnoses within 1 year per 1,000 examinations. ConclusionsLungAI contributes malignancy-risk information orthogonal to the size- and growth-based features underlying Lung-RADS and improves the allocation of screening patients across the existing management spectrum while potentially decreasing invasive procedures for benign lesions. Multi-site, multi-geography validation is required to demonstrate practical generalizability.

Detection of early-stage cancer using similarity-based classification of cell-free DNA fragmentation patterns

Wood, D. E.; Ballew, B. J.; Roy, J.

Current cancer screening efforts face the dual problems of low patient adherence and a lack of available tests for many cancers, which motivates the development of less invasive multi-cancer early detection strategies. Such strategies will likely involve the use of liquid biopsies, which can analyze biomarkers found in blood for the presence of cancer. We introduce Blackjack, a new detection approach that focuses on the fragmentation patterns of plasma-derived cell-free DNA (cfDNA). Blackjack characterizes a persons cfDNA fragmentation sites with a position probability matrix (PPM) that encodes information about the sequence context of the sites. For classification, Blackjack computes two features that represent the similarity of an individuals PPM to those of known healthy and cancer cohorts. With these two features, Blackjack then uses logistic regression to classify a new sample as being from a healthy individual or a person with cancer. In cross-validation experiments using two external whole genome sequencing datasets, Blackjack showed high performance, achieving a minimum area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of 0.993 and minimum sensitivity (Sn) of 91.8% (at 98% specificity). This high performance was also present for stage I (AUC 0.991, Sn 89.8%) and stage II cancers (AUC 0.993, Sn 90.2%), and across eight different cancers (minimum AUC 0.985, minimum Sn 80.0%). We demonstrate that Blackjacks accuracy is stable at low simulated sequencing depths ([~]0.24x), and that tissue of origin prediction using Blackjacks similarity measures is high (minimum 82% accuracy of the top two predicted tissues). Our findings show that cfDNA fragmentation site contexts have valuable signals for early cancer detection, and that developing diagnostics with machine learning can benefit from a focus on having a small number of informative features.

Large Language Models to Label CT Radiology Reports for Incidental Findings Found in Lung Cancer Screening: A Comparative Analysis

van der Graaf, F.; Antonissen, N.; Scholten, E. T.; Schalkx, H.; Prokop, M.; Jacobs, C.

ObjectiveTo compare LLM and prompting strategies for labeling Specific Incidental Findings (SIFs) occurring during lung cancer screening, in radiology reports. Materials and MethodsIn this retrospective study, 300 CT reports from a Dutch academic hospital were included between 19th March 2003 and 28th December 2020. GPT-5, GPT-4-Turbo, Llama-3.1-8B-INST, MedGemma-4B-IT, a keyword-based search method, and a radiologist labelled the reports for the presence or absence of nine SIFs. The radiologists scores were the reference standard. Each LLM was evaluated with four different prompting strategies: two without Chain-of-Thought (CoT), and two with CoT (one zero shot, one few shot). The automated labelling methods were evaluated against the radiologist primarily using Cohens Kappa, and additionally sensitivity, specificity, and F1 scores. ResultsGPT-5 achieved substantial or almost perfect agreement for seven out of nine SIFs: aortic valve disease, bronchiectasis, consolidation, coronary artery calcification, emphysema, abnormality in mediastinal lymph nodes, and thyroid abnormalities, with Cohen {kappa} values of 0.81 (0.70, 0.93), 0.97 (0.93, 1.00), 0.71 (0.62, 0.82), 0.92 (0.87, 0.96), 0.91 (0.84, 0.97), 0.71 (0.62, 0.80), and 0.92 (0.86, 0.98) respectively. For the remaining two SIFs, moderate LLM agreement was obtained with MedGemma-4B-IT and with GPT-4-Turbo with Cohen {kappa} values of 0.48 (0.40, 0.60), 0.42 (0.31, 0.58) for interstitial lung abnormalities and mediastinal mass respectively. The highest agreement was obtained with the non-CoT prompt strategies for most SIFs. ConclusionThis study demonstrated that LLMs can automatically label radiology reports with agreement ranging from almost perfect to moderate, but not one LLM - prompt combination achieved the highest agreement across all SIFs.

Serum brain-derived tau distinguishes acute neurotrauma diagnoses

Shi, R.; Svirsky, S. E.; Balogun, W. G.; Zeng, X.; Fedak, J. D.; Nafash, M.; Brodman, S. T.; Okonkwo, D. O.; Puccio, A. M.; Karikari, T. K.

Traumatic brain injury (TBI) and spinal cord injury (SCI) are two common forms of neurotrauma that can present with overlapping clinical features, complicating early diagnosis and management particularly in acute and mixed-trauma settings.1-3 Accurate and rapid biological differentiation remains an unmet need.4 We hypothesized that brain-derived tau (BD-tau), an ultrasensitive blood-based biomarker preferentially selective for tau protein originating from the brain, would distinguish TBI from SCI. In a multi-cohort assessment of 342 patients admitted to the neuro-intensive care unit (285 with TBI, 57 with SCI), and 22 controls, baseline serum BD-tau concentrations were markedly elevated in TBI compared with SCI, discriminating between the two populations with high accuracy (area under the curve [AUC]=0.91, 95%CI 0.87-0.96). In paired serum-cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) samples, serum BD-tau significantly outperformed CSF BD-tau for injury-type discrimination (AUC=0.95, 95%CI 0.89-0.99 vs. 0.80, 95%CI 0.71-0.89). Baseline serum BD-tau also showed superior discrimination of TBI from SCI compared with established blood biomarkers of neurotrauma, including neurofilament light chain, glial fibrillary acidic protein, and ubiquitin C-terminal hydrolase L1 (AUC=0.82, 95%CI 0.76-0.88; vs. 0.53, 95%CI 0.43-0.63; 0.67, 95%CI 0.58-0.76; 0.71, 95%CI 0.64-0.79; all DeLong p<0.001). Longitudinally, serum BD-tau remained persistently elevated in TBI, but not SCI, for up to 144 hours post-injury, with distinct temporal trajectories between injury types. Together, these findings indicate that serum BD-tau enables rapid and minimally invasive differentiation of TBI from SCI and may support early clinical decision-making, triage, and enrollment into injury-specific therapeutic trials, highlighting the potential of circulating biomarkers to improve precision diagnostics in acute neurotrauma care.

Examining the Clinical Utility of Deep Learning Models for Perivascular Space Segmentation in Alzheimer's Disease

Tang, S.; Younes, K.; Penukonda, E.; Hausle, I.; Thropp, P.; Lupo, J. M.; Tosun, D.

Open-source deep learning models promise scalable quantification of MRI-visible perivascular spaces (PVS), an emerging marker of glymphatic dysfunction in Alzheimers disease (AD). Whether these models remain valid when applied to heterogeneous AD cohorts and preserve ground-truth derived biological associations has not been established. We benchmarked five open-source nnU-Net-based PVS segmentation models (nnU-Net T1, nnU-Net T1+FLAIR, mcPVS-Net, MedNet-PVS, ADNI-PVS-Net) against manual labels in 60 multi-site ADNI-3 participants balanced across cognitively unimpaired, mild cognitive impairment, and AD dementia, and in 12 publicly available scans from the VAscular Lesions Detection and segmentatiOn challenge (VALDO) challenge. We evaluated overall, voxel, object, and lesion-wise Dice; stratified performance by region, diagnosis, scanner, PVS severity, and white matter hyperintensity (WMH) burden; and tested whether model-derived PVS burden reproduced ground-truth derived associations with AD-related biomarkers. Despite high internal performance, all models showed a specialist-generalist trade-off: the AD-trained ADNI-PVS-Net achieved highest internal performance (overall Dice = 0.58{+/-}0.14) but degraded most externally (Dice [&le;] 0.11), while MedNet-PVS and mcPVS-Net generalized externally (Dice 0.27-0.30) but systematically misestimated PVS burden. Models were relatively robust across scanner and diagnosis but inconsistent across PVS severity, and some misclassified up to 32% of WMH as PVS. Notably, only AD-trained models reproduced the expected PVS-amyloid biomarker relationship; other models returned a null result, despite comparable or superior Dice scores. These findings show that internal segmentation accuracy and external generalizability alone do not guarantee downstream inferential validity. Careful model selection and cohort-specific validation against manual labels should precede clinical inference in AD.
