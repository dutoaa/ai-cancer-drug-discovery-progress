Cytokine Signatures of Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor Myocarditis and the Modifying Role of Clonal Hematopoiesis: A Machine-Learning and Multi-Omics Study

Manchanda, N.; Chehayeb, R. J.; Hong, D.; Jeyashanmugaraja, A.; Singh, J.; Cross, J. L.; Vijayram, A.; Martinez, C. M.; Jayakrishnan, R.; Christofides, A.; Lin, D.; Im, Y.; VanOudenhove, J.; Biancon, G.; Ibrahim, E.; Ardasheva, A.; Lin, E. Y. S.; Levine, R.; Cheng, E.; Halene, S.; Hwa, J.; Smith, N.; Villani, C.; Zhu, H.; Jha, A.; Kwan, J. M.

BackgroundImmune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) myocarditis affects up to 2% of patients on ICI and has up to a 50% mortality. Clonal Hematopoiesis of Indeterminate Potential (CHIP) is a risk factor for ICI myocarditis. ObjectivesTo leverage a multi-omics approach to evaluate cytokines associated with ICI myocarditis, immunosuppression resistance, CHIP in ICI myocarditis and implicated pathways and cell type contributions. Methods236 cardio-oncology patients were enrolled, including 88 who were on ICI. Cytokine profiling from patients on ICI were sampled and analyzed using machine learning and explainable artificial intelligence. Single-cell multi-omics of peripheral blood from Yale (n=19), Stanford (n=25), and MGH (n=63) evaluated cell-type contributions of cytokines and implicated pathways. Validation of Yale proteomic analyses was done using the Stanford CYTOF dataset. ResultsTop features associated with ICI myocarditis included IL13, CXCL10, PDGF-A, CCL13, CCL17 and KITlg. Top KEGG pathways associated with ICI myocarditis and immunosuppression resistant ICI myocarditis included JAK-STAT signaling, IL17 signaling and lipid/atherosclerosis pathways. At the single-cell level, the consensus signals distinguishing myocarditis from non-myocarditis across cohorts was predominantly driven by module-level JAK-STAT, T-cell, and Toll-like receptor activation. Similar transcription gene programs were activated in CHIP carriers including modules implicated in the pro-inflammatory immune response, such as NF-kappa B, IL17, MAPK, and TNF pathways within CHIP cells spanning lymphoid (cytotoxic CD8+ NKT, and natural killer cells) and myeloid lineages. ConclusionsAcross cohorts, JAK-STAT signaling emerges as a convergent and potentially actionable axis in ICI myocarditis that is further amplified in CHIP carriers, supporting cytokine-guided risk stratification and JAK-STAT-directed immunosuppression as testable strategies.

Prediction of Subsolid Pulmonary Nodule Evolution from Baseline CT Using Temporal Imaging Models

Bondarenko, M.; Qi, K.; Nowroozi, A.; Kim, J.; Kunzang, B.; Lee, A.; Liu, J.; Tran, N.; Weng, S.; Vella, M.; Chaudhari, G.; Schnizler, T.; Innanje, A.; Chen, T.; Sohn, J. H.

BackgroundPrediction of subsolid pulmonary nodule (SSN) progression from baseline CT may improve risk stratification and surveillance planning, but prior approaches have largely relied on fixed follow-up intervals. MethodsThis retrospective single-center study evaluated interval-aware temporal imaging models for predicting future SSN growth and morphology across heterogeneous surveillance durations. A total of 24,946 longitudinal scan pairings derived from 2,543 clinician-reviewed SSNs in 426 patients were analyzed. A discriminative deep learning model predicted interval growth from baseline CT, segmentation masks, and interscan interval information, while a temporally conditioned generative model predicted future lesion morphology. ResultsThe discriminative model achieved an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.772 (95% confidence interval: 0.704-0.818), with sensitivity of 80.2% and specificity of 58.7% on the test cohort. The generative model predicted future lesion morphology with a Dice similarity coefficient of 0.706 {+/-} 0.186. Prediction performance decreased with increasing follow-up duration, although both models generalized across intervals ranging from months to years. ConclusionInterval-aware temporal imaging models enable the prediction of future SSN growth and morphology from baseline CT while accounting for variable surveillance intervals. These findings suggest a framework for time-aware, personalized risk assessment that may support individualized surveillance strategies and future AI-assisted management of pulmonary adenocarcinoma spectrum lesions.

Environmental toxic metal exposure and conditional mortality survival among US adults: a prospective cohort study

Fang, X.; Schwartz, J.

BackgroundHeavy metal exposure has been associated with mortality, but differences in early and median survival times remain unclear. We estimated associations of blood lead and mercury and urinary cadmium and arsenic at lower conditional survival-time quantiles. MethodsWe linked the 1999-2014 National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey to mortality records through Dec 31, 2019, including 24,338 adults aged 40 years or older. Weighted censored quantile regression estimated associations at the 10th, 25th, and 50th percentiles, adjusting for age and other confounders across five imputations. Mercury and arsenic analyses used 2003-2014 cycles; urinary-metal cohorts required observed estimated glomerular filtration rate [&ge;]60 mL/min per 1.73 mZ. FindingsOf 24,338 participants, 6,992 died. Doubling urinary cadmium was associated with 1.71-1.92, 1.50-1.67, and 1.35-1.53 years shorter conditional survival at the 10th, 25th, and 50th percentiles (n=7,061). Corresponding blood lead estimates were 0.63-0.71, 0.55-0.65, and 0.61-0.63 years shorter (n=24,021). Some median estimates were not estimable. Cadmium associations persisted after lead adjustment; lead associations did not persist after cadmium adjustment. Arsenic had no statistically significant association with mortality in this study. Mercury was associated with longer survival despite adjustment for fish or omega-3 intake, possibly reflecting residual confounding. InterpretationLow-to-moderate cadmium exposure was associated with shorter survival, with the largest absolute loss among adults who die earliest. These findings support continued efforts to reduce cadmium exposure, with particular benefit for adults most vulnerable to premature death. FundingNone. We prioritised prospective studies in adult populations and systematic reviews, supplemented by targeted searches for analyses of survival time. Previous studies have associated lead and cadmium exposure with higher all-cause and cardiovascular mortality. Evidence for mercury has varied across populations and exposure levels, and findings for low-level arsenic exposure in US adults have been less conclusive. Most cohort studies reported relative hazards, although earlier research examined metal-associated differences in longevity and used machine learning to investigate variation in lead-associated survival. Evidence remained limited on how associations with metal biomarkers differ between early and median conditional survival times in general populations. Added value of this studyAmong 24,338 US adults aged 40 years or older in NHANES 1999-2014, with mortality follow-up through 2019, we estimated associations of four metal biomarkers with the 10th, 25th, and 50th conditional percentiles of survival time after examination. Censored quantile regression provided estimands in years of life. In the urinary cadmium cohort of 7,061 participants with an estimated glomerular filtration rate of at least 60 mL/min per 1.73 mZ, a doubling of cadmium was associated with 1.71-1.92 years shorter to 10% mortality, compared with 1.35-1.53 years at the median. The cadmium associations persisted in NHANES III, although the largest estimate in that cohort was at the median. Lead and survival time associations were attenuated after adjustment for cadmium. Arsenic estimates were not significant. We observed positive association between blood mercury and survival time, which might reflect some residual confounding that we couldnt control for. Implications of all the available evidenceThe evidence supports continued population-level efforts to reduce cadmium and lead exposure. With CQR, we were able to estimate the survival differences in years of life and for earlier deaths, providing valuable information for public health messages. Future studies using repeated exposure measurements and evaluations of exposure-reduction policies are needed.

AToP: An Explainability Framework for Transformer-Based EHR Predictions Using Attribution-Guided Temporal Pattern Mining

Belouali, A.; Kharrazi, H.

ObjectiveTo develop and evaluate AToP (Attribution to Temporal Patterns), a post-hoc explainability framework that converts per-token Integrated Gradients (IG) from a Transformer into structured temporal pattern narratives. Materials and MethodsAToP was applied to a single-stream Transformer trained on MIMIC-IV v3.1 (218,196 patients; 12.6% unplanned 30-day readmission). It mines frequent episodes from salient-token subsequences in the training cohort, aggregates attribution among patients whose records contain each token or pattern, and collapses near-duplicates by patient overlap. Validation used masking, reversed-order analysis, token-level faithfulness tests, and KernelSHAP comparison. ResultsThe Transformer achieved AUROC 0.77 and PR-AUC 0.34. Population-weighted attribution emphasized high-prevalence codes with small per-patient impact. Carrier-conditional attribution, the mean among patients with the token, surfaced rare high-attribution psychiatric markers including psychosis, self-harm overdose, suicidal ideation (SI), and severe major depressive disorder (MDD). Pattern-level attribution identified within-visit co-occurrences and cross-visit trajectories not captured by token rankings. Risk-increasing patterns included psychiatric sequences ({MDD, SI} [-&gt;] SI) and oncologic progressions (cancer anemia [-&gt;] ascites). Obstetric and chest pain evaluation patterns were associated with lower predicted readmission. DiscussionThe framework provides two complementary views of model predictions: carrier-conditional aggregation highlights rare, highly attributed clinical features, while temporal pattern analysis identifies within-visit combinations and cross-visit trajectories missed by token-level rankings. Masking and visit-order reversal provided evidence of correspondence with model behavior, but pattern attributions do not represent unique effects or establish clinical validity. ConclusionAToP turns a Transformers attributions into visit-anchored temporal pattern narratives, showing which events, in which order, contribute to its readmission predictions.

Serum Raman spectroscopy as a blood-based triage test for bladder cancer: a retrospective proof-of-concept study

Milligan, K.; Smith, I.; Flugge, A.; Fakhrua Aldeen, N.; Shine, B.; Muldoon, C.

BackgroundBladder cancer remains one of the most common urological malignancies. Current diagnostic pathways rely heavily on cystoscopy, an invasive investigation with significant cost and resource implications. A rapid blood-based assay capable of improving patient stratification before cystoscopy could reduce unnecessary invasive investigations while maintaining diagnostic sensitivity. MethodsArchived serum samples from patients with histologically confirmed bladder cancer and symptomatic control subjects were analysed using dried-serum Raman spectroscopy. Raman spectra were incorporated into supervised machine-learning classification models to generate predicted probabilities of bladder cancer. Diagnostic performance was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis. ResultsThe study included 49 patients with bladder cancer and 97 symptomatic controls. Predicted probabilities of bladder cancer were substantially higher in cancer patients than in controls. The Raman classifier demonstrated excellent diagnostic discrimination (AUC approximately 0.90). Incorporation of age into the classification model produced little additional improvement (AUC approximately 0.91). ConclusionsMachine learning-assisted analysis of dried-serum Raman spectra demonstrates promising diagnostic performance for bladder cancer. Prospective evaluation is warranted to determine its role as a minimally invasive triage test within contemporary bladder cancer diagnostic pathways.
