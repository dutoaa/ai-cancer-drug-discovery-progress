Data-efficient machine learning for the detection of duodenal neoplasia: a multi-centre study

Di Lisio, F.; Soilleux, E. J.; Jaeckle, F.

BackgroundMachine Learning (ML) has shown promise in histopathological diagnosis, but rare tumours, like duodenal epithelial neoplasms, are challenging because large, labelled datasets are difficult to assemble. We propose a data-efficient ML pipeline for detecting duodenal epithelial neoplasia in whole-slide images. MethodsWe assembled a five-hospital dataset of haematoxylin and eosin-stained duodenal biopsy whole slide images (WSIs) at 10x magnification, comprising 8,027 normal and 401 neoplastic biopsies, including adenomas, carcinomas and neuroendocrine tumours. WSIs were divided into patches and encoded using the Hibou-B foundation model. We compared global standardisation with unsupervised site-specific standardisation of patch embeddings. Our pipeline combined principal component analysis, a Gaussian mixture model and a Random Forest classifier. We assessed generalisability using leave-one-hospital-out cross-validation across four hospitals and on an independent test set from a fifth hospital. ResultsIn leave-one-hospital-out cross-validation, the pipeline achieved a mean accuracy of 90%, sensitivity of 96%, specificity of 83% and an AUC of 0.96. On the external test hospital, it achieved 94% accuracy, 93% sensitivity, 95% specificity, and AUC of 0.97. Site-specific standardisation improved mean cross-validation accuracy by 20 percentage points compared with global standardisation, while the Gaussian mixture pipeline improved accuracy by 12 percentage points compared with mean pooling of patch embeddings. ConclusionsWe present a data-efficient ML approach for detecting duodenal epithelial neoplasia from WSIs. Strong performance was maintained across hospitals and on an independent external test cohort despite the limited number of neoplastic training cases, supporting the feasibility of foundation-model-based approaches for rare histopathological diagnoses.

Developing an OMOP-Standardized Prostate Cancer Database and Improving Data Quality Using NLP and PSA-Based Algorithms

Wang, J.; Jackson, J. C.; Garza, A.; Nalla, S.; Ninnemann, T.; Zhang, Y.; Kuo, Y.-F.

ObjectiveTo develop and evaluate an Observational Medical Outcomes Partnership-(OMOP-) standardized prostate cancer database from the University of Texas Medical Branch (UTMB) Epic Electronic Health Record (EHR) and improve data quality using natural language processing(NLP) and prostate-specific antigen-(PSA) based algorithms. Materials and MethodsWe built a data pipeline to transform UTMB Epic EHR data for patients identified through the Galveston Cancer Registry (2010-2021) into OMOP Common Data Model (CDM) v5.4. Data quality was assessed by comparing the OMOP-standardized data with Galveston Cancer Registry data using availability agreement, Cohens kappa and Intraclass Correlation Coefficient. NLP was used to extract PSA, Gleason score, and cancer stage from clinical text, and PSA-based algorithms were used to identify missing treatment and biochemical recurrence. ResultsWe extracted 815 analytic cases from UTMB EHR. Among them, 700 (85.9%) were complete and concordant with the cancer registry. PSA showed excellent value agreement. Structured Gleason score and stage data were sparse (n<20), but NLP greately improved capture. Treatment agreement was good compared with the cancer registry and improved slightly for radical prostatectomy after applying a PSA-based algorithm. Using PSA trajectories, we identified 60 cases of biochemical recurrence. DiscussionThe OMOP-standardized data from UTMB showed good agreement with the cancer registry. However, structured EHR fields incompletely captured diagnosis, pathology, and treatment details. NLP and PSA-based algorithms substantially improved data capture. Manual review also revealed errors in registry data, showing that OMOP-standardized EHR data can complement and help improve cancer registry quality. Conclusion: OMOP standardization combined with NLP and PSA-based algorithms improved prostate cancer data quality and research readiness. Lay SummaryElectronic health record (EHR) systems contain valuable long-term health information about cancer, but their use is limited by difficulties in data extraction, the lack of standard data formats, and missing information in structured fields. In this study, we built a data pipeline to convert EHR data into the Observational Medical Outcomes Partnership (OMOP) Common Data Model, a widely used standard format that facilitates data sharing and collaborative research. To address missing data, we used natural language processing (NLP, a computational technique that enables computers to interpret human language) to extract prostate-specific antigen (PSA) levels, Gleason scores (a measure of cancer aggressiveness), and cancer stages from unstructured clinical notes. We also applied algorithms that track PSA blood trends over time to identify unrecorded cancer treatments and detect cancer recurrence. Comparing this database with a cancer registry demonstrated that standardized EHR records provide accurate clinical information and long-term follow-up. While structured EHR fields lacked key tumor characteristics, NLP dramatically improved their capture. Tracking PSA trends identified previously unrecorded treatments and flagged 60 potential cancer recurrences. Overall, combining standardized EHR formats with NLP and PSA-based algorithms creates a rich database to support real-world prostate cancer research.

Language eligibility criteria and enrolled racial and ethnic composition in US clinical trials: a cross-sectional study

Tolas, A.; Du, L. Y.; Schutz, N.; Schmiedmayer, P.; O'Sullivan, J. W.; Johnson, A.; Longenecker, C. K.; Sarraju, A.; Rodriguez, F.; Yang, E.; Kim, D. S.

BACKGROUND: Federal policy now requires sponsors to submit diversity action plans for clinical trials, yet the modifiable features of trial design associated with enrolled composition remain poorly characterized. English-language proficiency requirements are written into eligibility criteria and recorded as free text in a public registry, making them visible and, in principle, amenable to revision, but their relationship to enrolled composition has been examined only within single disease areas and in small samples of trials. We examined whether these requirements and documented language accommodation were associated with the racial and ethnic composition of enrolled participants. METHODS AND FINDINGS: We conducted a cross-sectional study of interventional trials registered on ClinicalTrials.gov with at least one US site and posted results, using a registry snapshot retrieved in October 2026. The primary cohort comprised the 44,487 trials with exclusively US sites, of which 21,924 (49.3%) reported baseline race and 17,434 (39.2%) reported baseline ethnicity with counts that reconciled with the posted denominator. Both exposures were classified from free-text eligibility criteria using regular expressions validated against independent physician review of 150 trials. For the English-language requirement classifier, sensitivity was 88.2% and specificity was 99.2%. The outcome was the trial-level proportion of enrolled participants in each category, analyzed using generalized linear models adjusted for intervention class, therapeutic area, sponsor, phase, enrollment, sites, start year, US Census region, and the population composition of the trial's site states. An English-language proficiency requirement appeared in 5,736 trials (12.9%); its prevalence rose from 7.4% of trials starting in 2010 to 26.2% in 2023 (odds ratio [OR] per decade 2.91, P<0.001), whereas documented accommodation, an eligibility statement allowing participation in a language other than English (Spanish in 99.9% of flagged trials), remained rare (788 trials, 1.8%). The requirement was associated with lower enrolled proportions of Asian (OR=0.85, 95% CI 0.77-0.93, P=0.001) and Hispanic/Latinx participants (OR=0.85, 95% CI 0.79-0.91, P<0.001), with no evidence of an association for White participants (OR=0.99, 95% CI 0.95-1.04, P=0.75). Documented accommodation was associated with a higher proportion of Hispanic/Latinx participants (OR=3.23, 95% CI 2.82-3.71, P<0.001). Geographic adjustment did not change the statistical significance of the requirement associations and attenuated the accommodation association. The main limitations were the cross-sectional design, which precludes causal inference; the restriction of enrollment analyses to trials reporting baseline demographics, which were more recent than those without such reporting; and possible misclassification of automatically classified exposures. CONCLUSIONS: English-language proficiency requirements became more than three times as common between 2010 and 2023 and were associated with lower enrolled proportions of Asian and Hispanic/Latinx participants. Less common documented language accommodation was associated with a higher enrolled proportion of Hispanic/Latinx participants. Because these criteria are investigator-written and publicly recorded, they may be a tractable target for improving enrollment equity, although whether revising them changes who enrolls requires prospective evaluation

Decision models can offload structured clinical tasks from large language models

Ariel, D.; Laban, G.; Endelman, S.; Balicer, R.; Dagan, N.; Cohen, S.

Decision models like Jev read text and return probabilities, categories, or ordinal levels, with confidence scores for the latter two, at a fraction of large language models (LLMs) cost. We explored how Jev could be integrated into clinical workflows for all these output types: mortality risk estimation, categorical Choosing-Wisely decisions for patient questions, and ordinal colon cancer staging. With standard LLM-style inputs, Jev trailed five flagship LLMs: mortality AUROC was 0.822 versus 0.837-0.846; Choosing-Wisely accuracy, 87.7% versus 98.4-100%; and staging accuracy, 45.4% versus 94.2-100%. Developer-recommended input alterations improved staging accuracy to 75.8% with medical context, 82.1% with task decomposition, and 85.8% with both. None of Jevs staging errors had full confidence. Using Jev for fully confident staging decisions and LLMs for the rest was non-inferior to each LLM alone and cut flagship LLM costs by 33-44%. Prompted as designed, decision models may efficiently share structured clinical tasks with LLMs.

A Multi-Domain EEG Framework Integrating Spectral, Functional Connectivity, and Topological Features for Subject-Level ADHD Classification

Rohani, F.; Mostafavi, S.; Ebrahimifakhar, P.; Zomorrodi, R.; Mueller, A.

BackgroundAttention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) is a common neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by persistent symptoms of inattention, hyperactivity, and impulsivity that significantly affect cognitive, academic, and social functioning. Although electroencephalography (EEG) has emerged as a promising non-invasive tool for identifying objective neurophysiological biomarkers of ADHD because of its high temporal resolution and cost-effectiveness, many existing machine learning studies rely on single-domain EEG features, which may not adequately capture the complex functional organization of the brain. MethodsThis study proposes a subject-level EEG classification framework that integrates three complementary feature domains: spectral characteristics, weighted Phase Lag Index (wPLI)-based functional connectivity, and topological descriptors derived from persistent homology. EEG recordings from 121 subjects were preprocessed and segmented into overlapping windows for feature extraction. Window-level features were aggregated to generate subject-level representations. Recursive Feature Elimination (RFE) was employed to select the 110 most informative features from an initial set of 1,043 descriptors. Five machine learning classifiers, including Support Vector Machine (SVM), Random Forest, XGBoost, CatBoost, and Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), were evaluated using stratified five-fold cross-validation. An ablation study was further conducted to assess the contribution of each feature domain. Model interpretability was investigated using SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) and Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations (LIME), two widely adopted Explainable Artificial Intelligence techniques for interpreting machine learning models. ResultsAmong the evaluated classifiers, the SVM achieved the highest classification performance, yielding an accuracy of 80.17%, an F1-score of 80.95%, a balanced accuracy of 80.14%, a receiver operating characteristic area under the curve (ROC-AUC) of 86.72%, and a precision-recall area under the curve (PR-AUC) of 87.34%. The ablation study demonstrated that combining spectral, functional connectivity, and topological features consistently outperformed individual feature domains, highlighting the complementary nature of these representations. Explainable AI analyses further identified functional connectivity features as the primary contributors to classification performance while confirming the additional value of spectral and topological descriptors. ConclusionsThe proposed framework demonstrates that integrating spectral analysis, functional connectivity, and topological data analysis provides an effective and interpretable approach for subject-level ADHD classification from EEG recordings. The combination of recursive feature selection, multi-domain feature fusion, ablation analysis, and Explainable Artificial Intelligence establishes a transparent and reproducible pipeline that may facilitate the development of reliable EEG-based clinical decision-support systems for ADHD.

AI-Assisted Tissue-Based Antibody Interpretation in Autoimmune Encephalitis: Prospective Validation and Reader Study

Cao, Z.; Xie, D.; Zou, S.; Li, D.; Martinez-Martinez, P.; Zong, S.; Zhang, Z.

Tissue-based assays provide broad screening for neural autoantibodies but remain vulnerable to technical and reader-dependent variability. We evaluated an integrated workflow combining automated 3,3'-diaminobenzidine tissue-based assay processing, digital whole-slide imaging, and interpretable deep learning. Model development used 2,357 retrospective serum and cerebrospinal fluid images referred from more than 80 institutions and processed in a centralized laboratory, with reference labels established by expert-consensus tissue-based assay interpretation. A prospective validation cohort included 200 images from 200 unique patients (154 positive and 46 negative; 100 serum and 100 cerebrospinal fluid). The same images were evaluated by two expert and two novice readers in a fixed-order two-round study, with unaided interpretation followed by artificial-intelligence-assisted interpretation after a washout of at least two weeks. The standalone binary classifier achieved an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.922, accuracy of 86.5%, sensitivity of 85.7%, and specificity of 89.1% in prospective validation. Pooled reader accuracy was 90.4% in the unaided round and 97.4% in the artificial-intelligence-assisted round (McNemar P < 0.001), with specificity of 82.6% and 99.5%, respectively. Novice-reader accuracy was 83.3% unaided and 96.5% with artificial-intelligence assistance (P < 0.001), approaching unaided expert accuracy of 97.5%. Among tissue-based-assay-positive cases, reader accuracy for cytoplasmic/nuclear antigen-associated versus neural surface antigen-associated staining-pattern subclassification was 88.8% unaided and 94.0% with artificial-intelligence assistance (P < 0.001). These findings support further multicentre, cross-platform evaluation of artificial-intelligence-assisted neural autoantibody tissue interpretation. Abbreviated summaryCao et al. report prospective validation of an automated tissue-based neural autoantibody workflow combining digital imaging and interpretable deep learning. Artificial-intelligence-assisted reading was associated with higher interpretation accuracy, particularly among less-experienced readers, supporting further multicentre validation across laboratories and imaging platforms.
