Genomic Studies in Blood and Marrow Transplantation (GS-BMT): An NCI-CIBMTR genomic medicine initiative in hematopoietic cell transplantation

Gadalla, S. M.; Lee, N.; Wu, X.; Pirsl, F.; Schmidt, G.; Rafati, M.; Hutchinson, A.; Vogt, A.; Hicks, B.; Liu, J.; Wang, J.; Gaddam, S.; Li, S.; Saber, W.; Chanock, S.; Kraft, P.; Savage, S. A.; Hong, H. G.; Spellman, S. R.

BackgroundGenomic studies in hematopoietic cell transplantation (HCT) are limited by sample sizes and targeted approaches. MethodsThe Genomic Studies in Blood and Marrow Transplantation (GS-BMT) project is a collaboration between the National Cancer Institute (NCI) and the CIBMTR(R) designed to investigate recipient and donor determinants of HCT outcomes. We are generating genome-wide SNP-array genotyping for all samples, and sequencing and methylation profiling for selected subsets. We report study design and cohort characteristics and present two proof-of-concept analyses: comparison of conventional and machine learning prediction of overall survival after HCT in 9,262 patients with acute myeloid leukemia (AML), and assessment of inherited susceptibility to clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential (CHIP) among 19,750 HCT donors using polygenic scores (PGS). ResultsGS-BMT includes 30,718 donor-recipient pairs who received allogeneic HCT in the United States between 2000 and 2019. In AML, clinical variables alone provided modest prediction for post-transplant survival, with similar performance for Cox proportional hazards, random survival forest, and deep neural network survival models (C-index=0.63, 0.62, and 0.62, respectively). In donors, CHIP-PGS did not differ by age (p=0.76) but was modestly lower in unrelated vs. related donors (-0.06 standard deviation [SD]; p=0.002). In an illustrative analysis assuming risk of event increases by 50% or 80% per PGS-SD, the top 5.2% and 12.3% of the donors, respectively, would have at least twice the risk of a donor with the average PGS. ConclusionsGS-BMT establishes a large genomic platform aiming to improve individualized risk prediction, donor selection, and precision medicine in HCT.

Redefining Synovial Pathotypes in Psoriatic Arthritis through Multi-Modal Histology and Machine Learning (The PSYNOVIUM Study)

Hum, R. M.; Christofi, M.; Smith, S. L.; Tan, Y.; Shukla, R.; Marshall, L. M.; Smith, M.; Williams, A.; Orozco, G.; Viatte, S.; Buch, M.; Barton, A.; Ho, P.; Martin, P.

BackgroundSynovial inflammation in psoriatic arthritis (PsA) lacks a validated histological classification framework. Current approaches apply rheumatoid arthritis (RA) synovial pathotypes to PsA, which overlooks immunobiological differences between the diseases and limits the predictive power of tissue stratification. We aimed to define data-driven, PsA-specific synovial pathotypes to facilitate tissue-driven precision medicine. MethodsIn this cross-sectional study, ultrasound-guided synovial biopsies were obtained from 61 patients with PsA (n=22), seropositive RA (n=23), seronegative RA (n=10), and other inflammatory arthritides (n=6). Sections underwent multi-modal staining, including H&E, picrosirius red, and immunohisto-chemistry for CD3, CD20, CD68, CD138, and CD31. Whole-slide digital image analysis and automated pixel classifiers quantified cellular and stromal metrics. Unsupervised hierarchical clustering identified intrinsic PsA pathotypes, which were correlated with clinical disease activity, radiographic osteoarthritis (OA), and longitudinal treatment responses. FindingsWhile a subset of PsA patients shared an inflammatory signature with seropositive RA, unsupervised clustering of the PsA cohort revealed three distinct, novel pathotypes: Dense Myeloid (macrophage-driven), Fibro-Stromal (fibrosis and pauci-immune), and Angio-Lymphoid (hypervascular with significant CD138+ plasma cell infiltration). Pathotypes were independent of treatment and concurrent radiographic knee OA. The Fibro-Stromal pathotype correlated with significantly higher cutaneous psoriasis severity. Longitudinally, patients with the Fibro-Stromal pathotype exhibited a trend toward lower treatment response (44%) following standard interventions (methotrexate or TNF inhibitors) compared to the Dense Myeloid (50%) and Angio-Lymphoid (80%) pathotypes. InterpretationWe present the first computationally derived synovial pathotypes specific to PsA, revealing distinct plasma cell and fibrotic architectures not captured by RA-centric models. To enable clinical translation, we distilled these findings into a pragmatic, four-marker diagnostic decision tree (CD31, CD68, CD138, and picrosirius red) designed for routine histopathology workflows, providing a staging ground for biomarker-stratified clinical trials in PsA. FundingFunding from the NIHR Biomedical Research Centre: Manchester, Arthritis UK, the Wellcome Trust, Brit-PACT, the National Psoriasis Foundation, and the Medical Research Council. Research in contextO_ST_ABSEvidence before this studyC_ST_ABSThe potential clinical utility of tissue-based patient stratification is well established in rheumatoid arthritis (RA), where baseline synovial pathotypes (lympho-myeloid, diffuse-myeloid, and pauciimmune) have guided targeted biologic therapy in landmark precision medicine trials. However, psoriatic arthritis (PsA) exhibits a fundamentally distinct synovial microenvironment, characterised by unique vascular architecture, altered stromal fibroblast phenotypes, and distinct immune infiltrates intertwined with the IL-23/IL-17 cytokine axis. Consequently, attempting to classify PsA synovial tissue using established RA-derived scoring systems risks misrepresenting intrinsic disease biology. There is currently no validated, disease-specific histological classification system for PsA, which forces therapeutic decision-making to rely on an empirical, trial-and-error approach rather than underlying target tissue pathobiology. Added value of this studyTo our knowledge, this is the first study to define data-driven, computationally derived synovial pathotypes entirely specific to PsA. By leveraging multi-modal digital pathology and unsupervised machine learning, we deconstructed the PsA synovium into three biologically distinct pathotypes: Dense Myeloid, Fibro-Stromal, and Angio-Lymphoid. We demonstrated that the highly inflammatory PsA microenvironment is distinctly driven by CD138+ plasma cells rather than the CD20+ B cells typically observed in RA. Furthermore, we identified a striking tissue-level divergence: patients with the highly fibrotic, pauci-immune Fibro-Stromal pathotype exhibited the most severe cutaneous psoriasis and demonstrated a trend toward higher failure rates on standard therapies, independent of concurrent osteoarthritis. Crucially, we translated these high-dimensional computational findings into a pragmatic, four-marker diagnostic decision tree (incorporating CD31, CD68, CD138, and picrosirius red). Implications of all the available evidenceThis study establishes that PsA synovial pathology requires a dedicated, disease-specific classification framework. The proposed diagnostic decision tree can be integrated into routine clinical histopathology workflows without the immediate need for advanced spatial or single-cell transcriptomics. Furthermore, the correlation between the Fibro-Stromal pathotype, severe skin disease, and poor joint response to standard therapies provides a biological rationale for stratifying this specific subgroup towards early IL-17 or IL-23 inhibitor intervention alongside conventional synthetic disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs. Ultimately, these novel PsA pathotypes provide the vital staging ground required to design and execute biomarker-stratified, tissue-driven clinical trials, paving the way for precision medicine in PsA.

Plasma phosphorylated tau species across neurodegenerative and cerebrovascular diseases: An ONDRI study

Sanchez, E.; Connon, P.; Pasternak, M.; Abrahao, A.; Keith, J.; Dilliott, A. A.; Baril, A.-A.; Frank, A.; Lang, A. E.; Pollock, B. G.; Marras, C.; Fischer, C. E.; Seitz, D.; Dowlatshahi, D.; Grimes, D. A.; Tang-Wai, D. F.; Sahlas, D. J.; Rogaeva, E.; Finger, E.; Tan, K.; Binns, M. A.; Tartaglia, M. C.; Borrie, M.; Freedman, M.; Goncalves, R. A.; Swartz, R. H.; Hegele, R. A.; Dixon, R. A.; Mirza, S. S.; Farhan, S. M. K.; Black, S. E.; Kumar, S.; Pasternak, S. H.; Rajji, T. K.; Steeves, T.; Swardfager, W.; Zetterberg, H.; Kvartsberg, H.; Munoz, D. P.; Zinman, L.; Strong, M. J.; Masellis, M.

Elevated plasma phosphorylated tau (p-tau) species are proposed as specific biomarkers of Alzheimers disease (AD) pathology but have also been reported in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS). Their profile across neurodegenerative and cerebrovascular diseases, as well as their discriminatory value when combined with other plasma biomarkers, remain uncertain. We studied 551 participants (ages 40.1 to 87.1 years; 207 women), comprising 506 patients from the Ontario Neurodegenerative Disease Research Initiative (ONDRI) multi-site cohort and 45 amyloid-{beta} (A{beta}) PET-negative cognitively unimpaired controls from the Brain-Eye Amyloid Memory study. Ten diagnostic groups were studied: mild cognitive impairment (MCI), AD, Parkinsons disease subdivided into cognitively unimpaired (CU), MCI, and dementia, frontotemporal dementia (FTD), cerebrovascular disease (CVD) subdivided into cognitively unimpaired (CU) and cognitively impaired (CI), ALS, and the control group. Baseline visit plasma measurements included p-tau217, p-tau181, A{beta}42/40, neurofilament light chain (NfL), and glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP). Cross-sectional analyses compared biomarker levels as well as p-tau217-based inference of amyloid positivity status across diagnostic groups. Receiver operating characteristic-area under the curve (ROC-AUC) analyses ascertained the discriminatory capacity of these biomarkers and ratio combinations. Within the ALS group, baseline biomarker measures were also tested for association against baseline clinical features as well as longitudinal measures of neuropsychological change. P-tau217, p-tau181, GFAP, and NfL differed across groups (all P < 0.001). Compared to controls, p-tau217 was elevated in AD and ALS, p-tau181 and GFAP only in AD, and NfL in ALS and FTD. Inferred amyloid-{beta} positivity was more probable in MCI, AD, ALS, and cognitively impaired CVD groups compared to controls (all P < 0.05). The p-tau217 to NfL ratio discriminated ALS from AD with an ROC-AUC of 0.92 (95% CI 0.85-0.97) compared to p-tau217 alone at 0.65 (95% CI 0.52-0.76). No ALS clinical or longitudinal association survived correction for multiple comparisons. Elevated p-tau217 and inferred amyloid-{beta} positivity in ALS provide further evidence that the assay signal is not necessarily specific for AD pathology. However, the use of p-tau217 in combination with NfL provides improved discriminatory power between ALS and AD, supporting the use of plasma biomarker combinations rather than sole reliance on individual plasma biomarkers. Inferred amyloid-{beta} positivity in cognitively impaired CVD may indicate AD and/or cerebral amyloid angiopathy co-pathology. Currently available plasma biomarkers should therefore be used as diagnostic complements and interpreted within clinical context rather than as standalone molecular diagnoses.

Dense Breast Tissue Segmentation under Limited Annotation: Comparing CNNs, Transformers, Self-Supervision, and Prompting

Qu, B.; Liu, W.; Zhou, L.; Guo, X.; Malin, B.; Yin, Z.

Dense breast tissue reduces the sensitivity of mammographic screening and is an important risk factor for breast cancer, making accurate dense-tissue segmentation particularly important when expert pixel-level annotations are limited. We empirically study dense breast tissue segmentation under limited pixel-level supervision using a small expert-annotated dataset (596 images) together with an in-domain unlabeled mammography dataset (20,000 images). We compare classical pixel-level segmentation methods, CNN and U-Net encoder-decoder models, transformer-based models, self-supervised pre-training, fine-tuning strategies, loss functions, and prompt-based MedSAM variants under a unified evaluation protocol. CNN-based models achieve the best standalone segmentation performance while providing the most favorable balance between accuracy and computational cost. In contrast, the adaptive fuzzy c-means + SVM baseline and transformer-based models perform worse in this low-label setting. Our analysis of prompt-based MedSAM further shows that SAM-style models are highly sensitive to prompt quality. Box and breast-region prompts lead to poor performance, whereas CNN-generated dense prompts substantially improve MedSAM performance. However, even with mask-decoder fine-tuning, CNN-prompted MedSAM does not outperform the best standalone CNN model, suggesting that current SAM-style models require stronger prompts or adaptation beyond the mask decoder for diffuse breast-density segmentation. Additionally, a density-calibrated hybrid loss primarily improves percentdensity calibration by reducing the test MAE from 14.8 to 11.8 percentage points. Our computational cost analysis indicates that many generic self-supervised learning and parameter-efficient adaptation strategies provide limited practical benefit relative to their additional cost. These findings offer practical guidance for developing reproducible dense breast tissue segmentation models in mammography studies with limited labeled data.
