Development and Validation of an Automated Image-Based Method for Prognostic Stratification in Lung Adenocarcinoma using Immunohistochemical Markers

Carraro, F.; Sanguesa, M.; Irisarri, A.; Orive, D.; Sainz, C.; Arganda-Carreras, I.; Calvo, A.; Montuenga, L. M.; Ortiz de Solorzano, C.; Ariz, M.

We present an automated image-based framework for quantifying biomarker expression in immunohistochemical (IHC) images and introduce the Marker Expression Level (MEL) score as an objective alternative to visual H-scoring. MEL addresses key limitations of existing automated methods by integrating tissue-region segmentation with tumour-area-weighted marker quantification, normalization to mitigate inter-sample and inter-batch staining variability, and robust quantification of nuclear, cytoplasmic, and membrane-associated biomarkers. The complete pipeline was developed as an open-source Fiji/ImageJ tool and is publicly available. We evaluated MEL using IHC images from 128 lung adenocarcinoma patients stained for BRCA1, GLUT1, and QKI, three markers forming a prognostic signature previously described by our group. Tissue regions were segmented using a machine learning-based Fiji plugin, and MEL scores were calculated within tumour regions. A normalization strategy accounted for inter-TMA staining variability. Agreement with visual H-scores was assessed using Spearman correlation. Prognostic performance was evaluated using Cox regression to derive a Combined Prognostic Index (CPI) incorporating MEL scores and tumour stage. Segmentation achieved high accuracy across tissue regions (Dice coefficients 0.69-0.99), with lower performance in highly immune-infiltrated stromal areas. MEL scores significantly correlated with visual H-scores for all markers ({rho} = 0.61-0.69, p < 0.001). Normalization improved agreement for GLUT1 ({rho} = 0.39 to 0.62). The derived CPI stratified patients into distinct risk groups with significant differences in disease-free and overall survival (p < 0.05), comparable to visual scoring. MEL provides a reproducible and scalable alternative to expert visual H-scoring and standard semi-automated approaches such as QuPath. Its integration with prognostic modelling reproduced a validated survival signature with performance comparable to expert scoring, supporting clinical and translational applicability. MEL is not a marginal refinement of existing digital scoring methods, but rather a clinically oriented and biologically adaptable framework designed to improve the robustness and reproducibility of automated IHC quantification.

inClinico Agent for clinical trial endpoint prediction and prospective evaluation

Chen, J.; Duan, C.; Gou, W.; Wang, J.; Zheng, Z.; Leung, H.; Naumov, V.; Sidorenko, D.; Aliper, A.; Ren, F.; Pun, F. W.; Zhavoronkov, A.

High attrition rates across clinical drug development impose immense clinical and financial burdens, yet prospectively forecasting whether an individual trial will achieve its primary endpoint remains challenging. Here we present a machine-learning system that predicts clinical trial success across phases I-IV, independently modeling studies with efficacy versus safety/toxicity primary endpoints. To prevent lookahead bias and outcome leakage, models are trained on registration-time features, including target and mechanism-of-action precedent, drug modality and novelty, trial design and operational characteristics, and frozen literature-derived target-choice embeddings. Each prediction yields a continuous Predictive Trial Index (PTI) between 0 and 1 reflecting success probability. On a test set of trials initiated in 2021 or later, the efficacy model achieved an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) of 0.828 (95% CI 0.812-0.845; n = 2,347) and the safety/toxicity model reached 0.809 (95% CI 0.787-0.831; n = 1,485). These models are embedded in inClinico Agent, an agentic AI system integrating frontier large language models (LLMs) with deterministic scoring engines, retrieval tools and specialized clinical skills. We also introduce ClinicoBench, a prospective benchmark of 3,235 ongoing trials evaluated by inClinico Agent and five frontier LLMs, establishing a leakage-free foundation for tracking prospective predictive accuracy as trials reach completion.

Training-free generation of 4D CT and other rare acquisitions improves segmentation across modalities

Sadee, C.; Dils, A.; Hundal, N. S.; Wan, K.; Chun, A.; Shukla, A.; Xi, I. L.; Van Puyvelde, M.; Hartmann, K.; Shah, P.; Hirschvogel, M.; Zhou, W.; Lin, C.; Gangaram, V.; Er, A. G.; Seweryn, M.; Van Criekinge, W.; Nandi, T. N.; Aniegbe, J. I.; Shen, L.; Ilo, A. C.; Immel-Sadee, F.; Nnamani, A. O.; Levin, D. L.; Hao, M.; McDonald, E. S.; Achilefu, N. C.; Iloanusi, N. I.; Poplack, S. P.; Dashevsky, B. Z.; Damrauer, S. M.; Okoye, I.; Gevaert, O.

RationaleRoutine clinical imaging captures anatomy under a limited set of physiologic and acquisition conditions, leaving segmentation models with sparse examples of how lesions and surrounding tissues deform with compression, respiration, or vascular pulsation. Standard image augmentation expands training datasets but does not explicitly model the mechanics underlying these changes. Patient-specific biomechanical simulation offers an approach to generate anatomically consistent images and corresponding segmentation masks under conditions absent from the original scan, without additional imaging or training a generative model. We hypothesized that incorporating this mechanistically grounded variability into training improves segmentation across imaging modalities. MethodsWe evaluate across three segmentation tasks: breast cancer on 3D digital breast tomosynthesis, lung tumors on chest CT, and carotid plaque on ultrasound. From each routine scan, patient-specific biomechanical models generate acquisitions absent from the original study--a range of breast compressions, the respiratory cycle, and arterial wall distension across the cardiac cycle. The resulting displacement fields deform both images and masks. Segmentation models were trained with and without the generated data across a range of standard image augmentation techniques, evaluated via cross-validation and held-out test sets. Realism was assessed through blinded reader studies, and generated respiratory motion was validated against real 4D CT. ResultsTraining on the generated acquisitions improved segmentation across modalities, increasing Dice scores by 6.6% for breast (p<0.05), 10.1% for lung (p<0.05), and 4.5% for carotid plaque (p>0.05) relative to the routine scans alone. Generated respiratory motion closely matched real gated 4D CT, with Dice of 0.94 {+/-} 0.02 for lung volume overlap. Blinded readers discriminated generated from real images at near chance (AUC: breast 0.61, lung 0.41, carotid 0.46). ConclusionsMechanistic simulation generates imaging acquisitions that clinical systems do not supply, without training data or additional scanning, and improves AI-based disease detection across modalities.

Can Language Models Diagnose Sepsis? A Head-to-Head Evaluation Against 353 Physicians

Macias-Fassio, E.; Betancur, D.; Pruenza, C.; Giglio, A.; Gallano, G.; Borges-Sa, M.; Morales, A.; Fierrez, J.

ImportanceSepsis is a leading cause of in-hospital mortality, where timely and accurate diagnosis is critical yet frequently delayed. Whether large language models (LLMs) can diagnose sepsis reliably -- and whether they do so fairly, consistently, and with well-calibrated confidence -- remains unknown. ObjectiveTo evaluate the diagnostic capabilities of LLMs on standardised sepsis vignettes before and after biomarker disclosure, benchmarked against physicians, and to characterise their robustness, stability, bias and confidence calibration. DesignRetrospective in-silico study based on six anonymised real cases spanning the diagnostic spectrum from no infection to septic shock. For each case, the reference-standard diagnosis was assigned, following the Sepsis-3 framework. Each case was evaluated under a two-stage (pre- and post-biomarker disclosure) protocol. To assess potential demographic bias, every case was additionally presented while systematically varying the patients sex, age, and language. Setting and ParticipantsFive locally deployed LLMs (DeepSeek-4, Qwen3.8-27B, its quantised variant, Gemma-4-31B, and the domain-specific MedGemma) were evaluated in a controlled evaluation pipeline and compared with responses from 353 clinicians, using identical clinical vignettes. Main Outcomes and MeasuresDiagnostic accuracy before/after biomarker disclosure, per-vignette error, sensitivity to demographic attributes (diagnosis-change rate) and its directional component, reproducibility across repetitions and confidence calibration. ResultsBefore biomarkers, model accuracy ranged 40-67%, comparable to physicians (57%). After disclosure, physician accuracy was 61%, exceeded by Qwen3.8-27B (91%), its quantised variant (86%) and Gemma-4-31B (83%); DeepSeek-4 reached 64% and MedGemma 56%, the only model below physicians. Biomarkers rescued confirmatory cases (pancreatitis-associated sepsis), whereas post-surgical sepsis remained difficult for models and physicians alike. Quantisation cost only 5 accuracy points. Patient sex and age showed no directional bias, and language was the only bias. Gemma-4-31B was the most reproducible model, the Qwen models were the most accurate and best calibrated, and MedGemma the least. Conclusions and RelevanceLLMs, including quantised variants, can provide accurate informed decision support for sepsis, surpassing physicians once laboratory data are available. However, robustness, calibration, and language fairness varied independently of accuracy and should guide model selection and clinical validation.

Longitudinal AI Models Predict Cancer Adverse Events

Fu, C.; Yu, J.; Shi, R.; Waters, M.; Pavlak, M.; Arfe, A.; Shah, S. P.; Battle, A.; kehl, K. L.; Bot, B. M.; US Cancer AI Alliance, ; Schultz, N.; Morris, Q. D.; Jee, J.

Clinical interventions for cancer treatment toxicities can reduce morbidity, mortality, and healthcare cost, if high risk patients can be identified. We developed SPARC, a transformer-based foundation model, using real-world, longitudinal, routinely collected health measurements, to predict adverse event risk. Pre-training and fine-tuning SPARC on 101,920,379 measurements from 224,047 patients with cancer led to state-of-the-art performance at predicting cancer-related adverse events on (i) held-out data from the development cohort, (ii) a prospective validation cohort from the same center (Npatients=23,126), (iii) multiple publicly available external datasets (Npatients=26,012), and (iv) external, multi-hospital cohorts (Npatients=562,620, 206,840). SPARC generalized to multiple external centers and identified patients most likely to benefit from an intervention (G-CSF). Pooled multi-institution, more than single-institution, fine-tuning improved generalizability. We release SPARC as a community resource. Our results highlight the utility of AI time series models in oncologic management.

Establishment of an Anti-Nephrin Autoantibody Assay and the Predictive and Diagnostic Significance of Pre-treatment Antibody Titers in Pediatric Nephrotic Syndrome

Horinouchi, T.; Muto, M.; Ozeki, T.; Nagano, C.; Inoki, Y.; Ichikawa, Y.; Sakakibara, N.; Kiriyama, M.; Sakai, Y. T.; Morita Yamamoto, E.; Podolinnaia, V.; Kim, J.; Tanaka, A.; Atarashi, Y.; Nagatsuka, N.; Miwa, K.; Onishi, M.; Ganguly, A.; Kita, K.; Maharjan, B. D.; Yamamura, T.; Kamei, K.; Hamada, R.; Yamamura, N.; Goto, M.; Ban, H.; Kaku, Y.; Kuroki, R.; Fujita, N.; Miyano, H.; Kise, T.; Fujimaru, R.; Kaito, H.; Hidawa, E.; Ikezumi, Y.; Shimabukuro, W.; Okuda, Y.; Shima, Y.; Nada, T.; Akiyama, S.; Fujioka, K.; Miyanishi, M.; Ishikura, K.; Nakanishi, K.; Isaka, Y.; Maruyama, S.; Harada, A.;

IntroductionAnti-nephrin autoantibodies have been implicated in podocytopathies, including minimal change disease and focal segmental glomerulosclerosis. However, their prevalence, longitudinal dynamics, and clinical significance in first-onset nephrotic syndrome (NS) including steroid-sensitive NS (SSNS) and steroid-resistant NS (SRNS) remain unclear. MethodsWe established a highly sensitive anti-nephrin autoantibody assay using a modified recombinant nephrin antigen and the automated immunoassay platform, and applied it to a prospective Japanese pediatric, multicenter, cohort with first-onset NS and an independent validation cohort. Anti-nephrin autoantibody titers were evaluated before and after treatment and analyzed in relation to clinical phenotypes, genetic risk alleles, and tissue findings. The assay was validated using immunoprecipitation- Western blotting (IP-WB) in representative samples, assessing IgG-nephrin colocalization in evaluable kidney biopsy specimens, and antigen inhibition assays in all antibody-positive samples from the validation cohort. ResultsIn the pediatric discovery cohort (n = 103), all patients with SSNS (n = 83, 100%) and nine of 16 with SRNS (56.3%) were positive for anti-nephrin autoantibodies, whereas all patients with IgA nephropathy (IgAN) with NS onset were negative (n = 4, 0%). Standard-range positivity was associated with SSNS (77/77, 100%), high-titer positivity with SRNS (9/15, 60.0%), and antibody negativity was restricted to non-SSNS diseases, comprising SRNS (7/11, 63.6%) and IgAN (4/11, 36.4%) (P < 0.0001). Similarly, in the independent validation cohort (n = 42), high-titer positivity was associated with SRNS (1/1, 100%), standard-range positivity with SSNS (30/33, 90.9%), and antibody negativity identified IgAN and membranous nephropathy (5/8, 62.5%) or SRNS (3/8, 37.5%). Orthogonal validation by IP-WB, IgG-nephrin colocalization, and antigen inhibition assays was concordant. ConclusionsAnti-nephrin autoimmunity is a hallmark of NS and identifies distinct clinical phenotypes at disease onset. The automated immunoassay for anti-nephrin autoantibodies enables rapid, highly sensitive, and automated antibody measurement and may serve as a diagnostic triage tool for early etiologic stratification and clinical characterization of NS at disease onset. Lay SummaryNephrotic syndrome is a kidney disease in which proteins leak into the urine because of a damaged kidney filtration barrier. Recent studies suggest that some patients produce autoantibodies against nephrin, a key protein required for normal kidney filtration. However, accurately measuring these antibodies has been difficult. In this study, we developed a highly sensitive automated blood test for anti-nephrin autoantibodies and used it in Japanese children with newly diagnosed nephrotic syndrome. We found that all children with steroid-sensitive nephrotic syndrome were positive for anti-nephrin autoantibodies at disease onset. In contrast, children with very high antibody levels or no detectable antibodies were more likely to have steroid-resistant disease or other kidney diseases. These findings were confirmed in an independent validation cohort. This blood test may help improve diagnosis, predict treatment response, and support more personalized management of nephrotic syndrome.

Using Diffusion Similarity Networks to Identify, Track, and Predict Movement Disorders

Shalaby, A.; Chitta, K. K.; Alijanpourotaghsara, A.; Choi, J. W.; Hitti, F. L.; Chiang, F. L.; Sharma, V. D.; Kim, W. H.; Pouratian, N.; Lee, J.

Characterizing individual structural brain alterations in movement disorders like Parkinsons disease (PD) and Essential Tremor (ET) remains a significant challenge in clinical neuroscience. While brain structural connectivity (SC) derived from Diffusion Tensor Imaging (DTI) tractography maps physical white matter pathways, it fails to capture localized microstructural remodelings within gray matter structures. In this study, we use Diffusion Similarity Networks (DSNs) as a novel framework to estimate microstructural connectivity from diffusion weighted imaging (DWI). First, we evaluate the ability of DSNs to differentiate individuals with PD from healthy controls (HC). We used Similarity Network Fusion (SNF) to visualize differences between the fused networks of both groups as well as the Brain Connectivity Toolbox (BCT) to quantify changes in network topology, including transitivity, clustering coefficient, global efficiency, and betweenness centrality. Second, to evaluate the capacity of DSNs to track disease progression over time, we utilized longitudinal data extracted from the Parkinsons Progression Markers Initiative (PPMI) database, mapping distinct functional subnetwork degeneration trajectories in PD patients. Finally, we developed a predictive Graph Neural Network (GNN) framework on a locally collected cohort to address the challenging task of differentiating PD from ET, utilizing DSNs as highly discriminant input features. Our results demonstrate that DSN metrics successfully capture distinct structural signatures that reliably separate clinical phenotypes, while longitudinal modeling reveals an accelerated, progressive decay in specific motor and basal ganglia subnetwork topologies over time. Furthermore, the GNN model achieved high classification performance (Balanced accuracy = 0.8684, AUC= 0.9420) in distinguishing PD from ET globally, with local explainability analysis successfully showing the specific brain Regions of Interest (ROIs) driving the differential diagnosis. Overall, these findings demonstrate that Diffusion Similarity Networks serve as a robust, non-invasive biomarker for movement disorders, offering deep insights into disease progression and an explainable AI tool for clinical decision support.

A family-aware All of Us benchmark for polygenic prediction and genomic machine learning

Ren, G.; Wu, K.; Ren, C.; Nyiha, I.; Wu, K.; Yang, J.

Polygenic risk scores (PRSs) have improved substantially in predictive performance, yet results remain difficult to compare across studies that differ in cohorts, phenotype definitions, and evaluation procedures. Comparison to machine-learning methods is harder still, since their performance depends strongly on how data are partitioned and whether related individuals leak across splits. Here, we introduce a family-aware benchmark for polygenic prediction built on version 9 of the All of Us Research Program (AoU). The benchmark assigns entire family components to fixed discovery, calibration, and test partitions, reserves first-degree relatives for within-family evaluation, and separates models trained only on AoU discovery data from those using external GWAS summary statistics. This enables direct comparison between conventional PRS pipelines and machine-learning models trained under identical data budgets. We report results for 14 binary disease traits. Our strongest scores explain 5.7-24.9% of liability-scale variance, with particularly strong prediction for Alzheimers disease, type 2 diabetes, prostate cancer, glaucoma, and inflammatory bowel disease, and match or exceed published commercial and academic scores for most traits. Across 12 traits with interpretable within-family estimates of within-sibship-to-population effect ratio, 11 confidence intervals included a ratio of 1. Type 2 diabetes showed an attenuation ratio of 0.86 [0.76, 0.97].

Exhaustive white blood cell extraction and profiling across whole-slide images of blood and bone marrow smears

Deutges, M.; Ghete, T.; Namsaraeva, A.; Sembill, S.; Metzler, M.; Marr, C.; Buser, M.

Blood and bone marrow smears are central to diagnosing hematological disorders. While whole-slide scanning enables automated single-cell profiling at scale, the computational burden of gigapixel images has confined automated analysis to manually pre-selected regions of interest. Here, we present HemAutomaton, a framework built on neural cellular automata that extracts white blood cells across entire whole-slide images without manual region selection and with minimal annotation requirements. HemAutomaton matches the in-domain detection accuracy of Faster R-CNN while surpassing it under domain shifts, operating at a fraction of the computational cost and with over 10,000-fold fewer parameters. Across three blood cancer cohorts, we extract over 100 million single-cell images and achieve annotation-efficient differential counting via few-shot classification using a pretrained hematology foundation model. Slide-wide spatial analysis uncovers consistent blast-frequency gradients across smears and demonstrates that automated blast counts match expert evaluations within inter-expert variability. Scaling automated profiling from hundreds to tens of thousands of cells per smear overcomes conventional sampling limits and establishes the foundation for more sensitive AI supported hematological assessments.
