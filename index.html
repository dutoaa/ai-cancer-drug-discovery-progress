Pathology foundation models for training-free case retrieval in salivary gland tumors

Maximilian J. Roesel, Niklas Kiermeyer, Philipp Keyl, Frederick Klauschen

In pathology, pattern recognition relies on a frame of reference built through extensive training and experience, which may still fall short when encountering rare cancer subtypes. Archives of previously diagnosed tumors often contain relevant precedents, yet identifying the most relevant cases remains challenging. Here we explore whether content-based retrieval with current pathology foundation models facilitates rare cancer diagnostics without any task-specific training. Pathologists can query a digitized archive using a region of interest (ROI) from a whole slide image (WSI) and retrieve morphologically similar cases, along with their ICD-O codes and corresponding WSIs. The ROI can be selected manually or automatically using a gated attention-based multiple instance learning model. We evaluate our approach by leave-one-case-out on an imbalanced dataset of 137 salivary gland tumor cases spanning 12 ICD-O morphology categories, comparing four pathology foundation models (Atlas2, Atlas2-B, Atlas2-S, and UNI2h). Top-1/3/5 retrieval was 62.5%/76.7%/81.3% for Atlas2, 50.2/66.6/72.9% for UNI2h, 52.3%/71.5%/77.6% for Atlas2-B and 54.4/65.2/73.7% for Atlas2-S. Macro-averaged F1 ranged from 69.0% for the smallest model Atlas2-S to 76.6% for the largest model Atlas2 across the five classes with at least ten cases. Our modified Chamfer similarity separated correct from incorrect matches among the Top-5 more reliably for Atlas2 (AUC 0.754) than for Atlas2-B (0.679, p = 0.020) and Atlas2-S (0.653, p = 0.003), but not UNI2h (0.697, p = 0.196). Requiring no additional training or manual annotation, this approach makes departmental archives searchable with pathology foundation models, providing a low-barrier route to case-based differential diagnosis and extending the individual frame of reference to the collective experience of the department.

Can GPT Be Used as an Alternative Prediction Model to Traditional Machine Learning and Neural Networks on Low-Volume Clinical Data?

Bin Akter, S.; Akter, S.; Eisenberg, D.; Hill, C.; Lotvola, A.; Fresneda Fernandez, J.; Sarkar Pias, T.; Rafiqul Islam, M.; Islam, H.

Background and Objective: Early and reliable disease prediction from structured clinical data remains challenging when datasets are small, highly imbalanced, and contain limited positive disease cases. Conventional machine learning (ML) and deep learning approaches often struggle to capture clinically meaningful relationships under such low-data representation conditions due to weak statistical associations between features and prediction targets. This study proposes a clinically grounded GPT2-based table-to-text framework for disease prediction using structured healthcare datasets, motivated by the contextual reasoning capability of GPT models to better capture clinically meaningful relationships when statistical learning alone becomes insufficient due to limited data availability. Methods & Materials: Structured clinical records were transformed into physician-style textual descriptions and enriched through GPT4-generated medical paraphrasing to improve minority-class representation while preserving clinical meaning. Both the original and generated clinical texts were used to fine-tune a GPT2 model across four public healthcare datasets, including heart disease, heart failure, chronic kidney disease, and thyroid cancer recurrence. Gradient-based explainable AI analysis was additionally incorporated to identify clinically important features influencing prediction outcomes. Results: The proposed framework demonstrated consistently strong predictive performance with average precision, specificity, sensitivity, and F1-score of 0.96, 0.97, 0.96, and 0.96, respectively. The model achieved improved sensitivity, stronger generalization, and more stable predictive behavior compared with traditional ML, deep learning, transformer-based, and GAN-augmented approaches. Importantly, the framework consistently emphasized clinically meaningful variables even under severe class imbalance where conventional ML and neural network models often struggled. Conclusions: The proposed GPT2-based table-to-text framework provides a practical and clinically interpretable approach for disease prediction from limited structured healthcare data. By integrating contextual clinical reasoning with explainable prediction mechanisms, the framework demonstrates strong potential for early risk detection, transparent clinical decision support, and reliable deployment in real-world low-resource healthcare environments.

GraphBEAT: Graph-based Bucketized Event Aggregation Over Time for Early Risk Stratification of Gastrointestinal Cancers

Leo Y. Li-Han, Chris Varghese, Elizabeth B. Habermann, Mark J. Truty, William R. G. Perry, Cornelius A. Thiels

Longitudinal electronic health records (EHRs) contain complementary diagnostic and laboratory information about patient health. However, irregularity, sparsity, heterogeneity, and incompleteness in documentation complicate EHR modeling. To address the problem, we introduce GraphBEAT (Graph-based Bucketized Event Aggregation over Time), a temporal graph representation learning framework that groups clinical events into time buckets and transforms observed features into patient-specific, temporally directed graphs to enable early risk stratification for gastrointestinal cancers. Each node in an EHR graph encodes feature identity, observed value, and temporal position of clinical events. A context-aware, time-decay message-passing mechanism models transitions between successive buckets, followed by multi-head attention pooling to generate patient-level representations. GraphBEAT was trained and evaluated on multi-site longitudinal diagnosis codes and laboratory tests to predict pancreatic, liver, and intestinal cancer at 12-, 24-, and 36-month diagnostic lead times. In all experimental setups, GraphBEAT consistently outperformed baseline graph neural networks (GraphSAGE, GAT, and GCN) and sequence modeling approaches (Transformer and long short-term memory [LSTM]). GraphBEAT also required over 250x fewer floating-point operations per patient and over 20x fewer parameters than the Transformer and LSTM baselines. These results support GraphBEAT as an accurate and computationally efficient approach for cancer risk stratification from sparse, heterogeneous longitudinal health records.

MADDIE: Multi-Agent Drug Discovery Engine

Ye Liu, Reza Averly, Avery Meyer, Frazier N. Baker, Ruoxi Gao, Daniel Adu-Ampratwum

Drug discovery remains a resource-intensive and time-consuming process, involving billions of dollars and a decade of development to advance a drug to market. While many in silico tools have accelerated this process, operating them still demands substantial time, effort, and expertise from medicinal chemists. Autonomous systems built on Large Language Models (LLMs) have emerged as a promising solution, yet current designs limit their performance. Existing single-agent systems scale poorly to long-horizon, multi-step, and realistic tasks, whereas multi-agent systems typically execute predefined workflows that cannot capture the iterative, non-linear nature of a real drug discovery campaign. Here, we present MADDIE, a multi-agent drug discovery engine. It comprises a central agent, Orchestrator, and four specialized worker agents: HitIdentifier, LeadOptimizer, Synthesizer, and Evaluator, each representing a critical step in a drug discovery campaign. Under Orchestrator's orchestration and coordination, these worker agents are composed into flexible workflows, enabling MADDIE to adapt its strategy and navigate the drug discovery pipeline end-to-end. Across 50 protein targets, MADDIE produces molecules satisfying key property requirements and their synthesis routes, outperforming existing agentic systems, generative models, and general-purpose LLMs by a wide margin. Our analysis reveals how these agents collaborate and that MADDIE can employ different strategies across targets to achieve the campaign's goal, while still being efficient in time and tokens. Together, they demonstrate MADDIE as a low-cost, effective, and efficient autonomous engine for accelerating drug discovery. Code and dataset is available via https://github.com/ninglab/MADDIE.

Reinforcement Learning In Partially Verifiable Environments for Colon Cancer Treatment Planning

Li-Ching Chen, Andrés Franco Valiente, Yuta Ishiyama, Zachary R. Chalmers, William Chen, Kelsey Natsuhara

Clinical decision-making is a sequential process of information collection, clinical inference, and forecasting under partial observability, often without a uniquely correct treatment decision. Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated strong medical question-answering capabilities but extending these advances to clinical reasoning remains challenging because many clinical tasks are only partially verifiable. Recent clinical reinforcement learning (RL) work has therefore largely focused on diagnostic endpoints, where correctness can be more directly verified. Oncology treatment decision making is a particularly challenging instance of this problem that extends beyond medical question-answering in which multiple decision trajectories can be clinically defensible for the same partially observed patient state. We formulate oncology treatment planning as an interactive RL problem under partial observability and construct patient-specific, process-aware rewards from decision processes observed in real world clinical records. The agent must learn to sequentially acquire information from a structured patient representation before committing to a first-line treatment recommendation, and is evaluated on information gathering, treatment selection, and reasoning alignment. Using GRPO and GDPO to optimize Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct within this environment, RL substantially improves the base policy beyond frontier model capabilities. The GDPO-trained policy increases Mean@3 scalar clinical reward by $79.60\%$ over base Llama and exceeds GPT-5.2 by $18.82\%$ under the same interactive environment, while reducing query count during training. Overall, our results show that patient-specific, partially verifiable rewards can provide an effective learning signal for interactive clinical RL when both the patient state and the underlying clinical decision process are only partially observed.

Spectral Considerations on Unsupervised Time-domain EEG Representation Learning with Deep Networks

David Aquilué-Llorens, Abel Puigseslloses, Angel Blanco, Claire Braboszcz, Aureli Soria-Frisch

Electroencephalography (EEG) is a non-invasive, scalable source of quantitative biomarkers for the early diagnosis of brain disorders, and deep representation learning has become a powerful paradigm for extracting information-rich latent features from raw signals. However, neural networks tend to under-represent high-frequency information, a concern that is especially acute for EEG given its oscillatory nature and characteristic $1/f^χ$ power decay, which risks masking disease-relevant high-frequency signatures. In this study, we systematically investigate the spectral constraints of unsupervised time-domain feature extraction across three autoencoder architectures (LSTMVAE, VAEEG, Transformer) and a state-of-the-art EEG foundation model. Using synthetic experiments and empirical validation, we show that while autoencoders capture macro-scale shifts such as alpha slowing, they remain biased against higher frequencies due to a magnitude-driven masking effect induced by the Mean Squared Error loss under 1/f-decaying spectra. Increasing latent dimensionality mitigates this attenuation but sacrifices latent compactness and generative stability. We argue that, as the field advances toward large-scale foundation-level feature extractors, it is imperative to move beyond simple time-domain reconstruction and to integrate spectral-domain loss terms and gradient-balancing techniques, so that the subtle high-frequency transients critical for diagnosing complex brain disorders are preserved in the latent representation.

When do quantum views help? Complementary classical-quantum representations in multi-view fusion

Azadeh Alavi, Fatemeh Kouchmeshki, Hossein Akhoundi, Yongli Re, Abdolrahman Alavi, Muhammad Usman

Recent studies have reported promising results from quantum machine learning in drug discovery, healthcare, materials science and cybersecurity. Research includes comparisons with classical machine learning algorithms and the development of sequential hybrid pipelines. We propose that fusion can improve prediction when classical and quantum representations capture complementary predictive relationships. Our classical-quantum multi-view framework preserves the full classical input alongside simulated quantum features. Concatenation and attention combine the views; single-view comparisons, replacement and masking controls assess both views' contributions. Controlled angular-polynomial experiments distinguish targets adequately represented by a single view from those that depend on complementary angular and polynomial relationships. For these mixed targets, fusion improves prediction by combining the relevant feature sets, while matched classical representations recover the same benefit, linking the improvement to their complementary predictive content. Controlled classification tests further show that gains depend on alignment between the chosen representation and the target, and on a suitable fusion rule. Benchmark validation shows improved mean predictive performance over classical baselines on Wine, Wisconsin Diagnostic Breast Cancer, three-class Fashion-MNIST and Steel Plates Faults within the evaluated protocols. Classical replacement and masking controls remain competitive, classical baselines lead on Digits and Covertype, and outcomes with missing or corrupted views depend on the available inputs. These findings support evaluating quantum representations for their complementary predictive contribution and matching the representation and fusion strategy to the target relationships.

Robust Reinforcement Learning for Dynamic Treatment Regimes under Model and Measurement Uncertainty

Chengze Xie, Xuan Wang, Ying Ding, Hairong Wang, Yan Li

We develop a robust reinforcement learning (RL) framework for learning adaptive treatment policies that generalize across patients and remain reliable under errors in the measured biomarker and shifts in the disease dynamics. By modeling the patient as a hidden context, we address generalization and robustness in a unified manner through robust contextual Markov decision processes. Applying our framework to adaptive therapy for metastatic prostate cancer in a simulator calibrated to clinical data, we obtain generalizable and robust policies that substantially outperform the existing adaptive therapy method. In particular, we attain robustness with almost no loss of performance when the biomarker is accurate and the disease evolves as anticipated, and find that the resulting policy Pareto-dominates the existing method across a diverse array of scenarios. Our findings suggest that robust RL holds great promise as a principled methodology for learning reliable adaptive treatment policies.

Beyond the Encoder: Object-Centric Representations Emerging from Segmentation Foundation Models

Michalis Pistos, Bartłomiej W. Papież

Foundation models are increasingly used as feature extractors for downstream tasks with limited data, typically through representations obtained from their encoders. However, foundation models trained for structured tasks such as segmentation also form object-specific representations during task execution, whose transferability remains largely unexplored. We investigate Segment Anything Model 2 (SAM2) object pointers, compact object-level representations produced through the segmentation pathway, as transferable representations for downstream tumour prediction. Through experiments on two PET/CT tumour prediction tasks, we find that object pointers learned using segmentation supervision alone already encode tumour-discriminative information, despite never being optimised for the downstream labels. We further introduce task-guided object pointers, where an auxiliary objective jointly shapes the object pointer space for the downstream prediction together with segmentation. Task-guided pointers improve cancer-type classification, outperforming conventional encoder-based transfer and radiomics, while segmentation-only pointers perform best on the more challenging recurrence prediction task. Representation analyses reveal associations between object pointers and interpretable radiomics features, class-dependent organisation of the learned representation space, and explicit spatial grounding around the segmented tumour. Together, these findings suggest that useful transferable representations can emerge through the execution of a foundation model's primary task, providing an alternative to conventional encoder-based transfer.

Morphology-aware deep learning predicts colorectal cancer survival from whole-slide images

Hikmat Khan, Metin N. Gurcan, Anil Parwani, Wendy L. Frankel, Wei Chen, Muhammad Khalid Khan Niazi

Background: Five-year survival prediction from hematoxylin-and-eosin whole-slide images could support risk stratification, but the task is weakly supervised, and the tissue morphologies carrying prognostic information remain incompletely characterized. Methods: We conducted a retrospective secondary analysis using TCGA-COAD/READ (n=562) for morphology-specific analysis, Alliance/ CALGB 89803 (n=424) for model development, and SurGen (n=218) for external evaluation. We propose MorphContrast, a morphology-aware contrastive multiple-instance learning framework that uses tissue labels as structural supervision for five-year survival prediction. MorphContrast combines gated-attention pooling with three auxiliary objectives: patch-level morphology labels define within-patient contrasts, outcome concordance or discordance defines cross-patient contrasts within matched morphologies, and diversity regularization discourages representational collapse. Morphology-matched patches from patients with discordant outcomes serve as hard negatives, encouraging prognostic discrimination within comparable histologic contexts. Results: Morphology-specific models show weak but distributed prognostic discrimination: adipose tissue achieves an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of 0.622, stroma achieves 0.604, and both perform comparably to tumor epithelium (0.601-0.605). On Alliance/CALGB 89803, the single model achieves an AUC of 0.642, while the post hoc ensemble reaches a pooled out-of-fold AUC of 0.70 and a risk-group hazard ratio of 3.79 (95% confidence interval: 2.55-5.63). On the external SurGen colon cancer cohort (n=218), the single model and ensemble achieve AUCs of 0.669 and 0.678, respectively. Conclusions: These findings support tissue morphology as structural supervision for outcome-aware representation learning in colorectal cancer prognosis.

Dyachenko, A. A.; Bogdanov, M. A.; Bogdanov, D. V.; Meldo, A. A.; Chernina, V. Y.; Gombolevskiy, V. A.; Grjibovski, A. M.; Valkov, M. Y.

BackgroundCancer patients face a significantly elevated risk of severe COVID-19 outcomes. Although vaccination is a cornerstone of prevention, data on vaccination coverage and association with survival among Russian cancer patients remain scarce. We assessed COVID-19 vaccination coverage and its association with overall survival in a population-based cohort of cancer patients in Northwest Russia. MethodsThis retrospective registry-based cohort study integrated data from the Population-Based Cancer Registry (PBCR) of the Arkhangelsk Region and the Nenets Autonomous Okrug, regional vaccination records, and an artificial intelligence (AI)-based analysis of chest computed tomography (CT) scans. The cohort consisted of 1,144 cancer patients who underwent CT between August 2020 and April 2021. Vaccination status was ascertained via data linkage. Overall survival (OS) was compared between vaccinated and unvaccinated patients using Kaplan-Meier analysis and multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression, adjusted for demographic, clinical, and AI-derived CT biomarkers. To mitigate immortal time bias, the primary analysis used a landmark approach restricted to patients alive on March 1, 2021 (N = 1,136). A sensitivity analysis used a November 1, 2021 landmark (N = 894). Vaccination status was defined as receipt of at least one dose before the landmark date. ResultsOnly 6.6% (n=76) of patients received at least one dose of a COVID-19 vaccine (Sputnik V or Sputnik Light). Vaccinated patients were more likely to have earlier-stage cancer and were less likely to be receiving active treatment. In the primary landmark analysis, vaccination was associated with a significantly higher 3-year OS (80.3% vs. 45.7%, p < 0.001). In multivariable analysis, vaccination was independently associated with a 60% lower hazard of all-cause mortality (adjusted HR 0.40, 95% CI 0.25-0.64). In the sensitivity analysis (November 1, 2021 landmark), the adjusted HR for vaccination was 0.56 (95% CI 0.34-0.92), showing a consistent but attenuated association. ConclusionThis study reveals critically low COVID-19 vaccination coverage among cancer patients in Northwest Russia. Nevertheless, vaccination was strongly associated with a substantial survival benefit, underscoring its importance as a potentially lifesaving intervention for this vulnerable population.

MFG: Multi-turn Feedback Guided Molecular Optimization

Multi-objective molecular optimization is a critical process in drug discovery that aims to modify a given molecule to simultaneously improve multiple properties while maintaining a similar overall structure.
